## Legal-control determinations

1. The complaint should not assert that federal regulation creates an independent private cause of action unless a separately cognizable basis is verified.
2. Federal regulatory provisions may be relevant to context, materiality, notice, record existence, standard-of-care questions, or requested discovery, but only after applicability and event-date verification.
3. The federal definition of `donor` does not establish that a transaction is unpaid, charitable, or noncommercial.
4. Federal law expressly addresses Source Plasma donated by paid donors; compensation alone does not establish illegality.
5. A physician-oversight theory must identify the precise activity, actor, role, delegation rule, applicable exception, and documentation defect.
6. The proposition that physical physician presence is always required is not supported by the reviewed current framework and must not be pleaded categorically.
7. The proposition that signature-only oversight is always legally insufficient remains on hold pending event-specific proof and binding authority.
8. Every cited federal provision must be mapped to a specific state-law element or evidentiary purpose and must not be treated as negligence per se or automatic liability without verified authority.
9. Requested injunctive relief must be checked for conflict with FDA licenses, approved programs, and federal requirements.
10. Removal, preemption, and primary-jurisdiction analysis remains incomplete until binding Eighth Circuit, District of Minnesota, Minnesota appellate, and Minnesota Supreme Court authority is researched and recorded.
