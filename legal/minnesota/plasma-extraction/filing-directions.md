# Filing Directions — Minnesota Compensated Plasma Extraction Complaint

## STATUS

This is a filing-form package, not a representation that every allegation is presently proved. It is suitable for service only after the mandatory factual and procedural gates below are completed. The remaining blanks cannot lawfully be guessed.

## 1. DO NOT SERVE UNTIL THESE FACTS ARE VERIFIED

Complete each item in the complaint:

1. Plaintiff’s legal name.
2. A United States mailing address where service can reliably be received.
3. Telephone number and email address, if any.
4. Every facility address.
5. Every material participation date or a supportable date range.
6. The exact statements seen or heard, who made them, how they were delivered, and approximate dates.
7. The exact omitted information claimed to be material.
8. Concrete reliance: what decision was made because of the representation or omission.
9. Concrete injury and amount: transportation costs, testing costs, records costs, lost work, or another legally cognizable loss.
10. Laboratory values, deferrals, diagnoses, and medical causation only where supported by records or qualified expert review.

Delete any allegation that is not personally true or supportable after reasonable inquiry. Do not sign first and investigate later.

## 2. VERIFY DEFENDANT IDENTITIES

On the same day service is arranged, search the Minnesota Secretary of State business registry and verify:

- exact legal entity name;
- entity type;
- active status;
- registered office;
- registered agent, if listed;
- assumed names used in Minnesota.

The draft presently uses “BioLife Plasma Services L.P.” and “Octapharma Plasma, Inc.” These names must be confirmed. Do not serve a brand name when the operating legal entity is different.

Save the entity-detail pages as PDF exhibits for the service file. Do not place private account information in the public court filing.

## 3. SELECT THE CORRECT COUNTY AND JUDICIAL DISTRICT

Minn. Stat. § 542.09 generally permits venue where a defendant resides or maintains a business place, or where the cause of action or part of it arose.

- Saint Paul and Maplewood are in Ramsey County, Second Judicial District.
- Hennepin County is the Fourth Judicial District.

Use Ramsey County only when at least one material representation, procedure, injury, or Defendant business place supplies a Ramsey County basis. Use Hennepin County only when verified facts supply a Hennepin County basis. Change the caption, jurisdiction allegations, and Civil Cover Sheet consistently before service.

Official authority: Minn. Stat. § 542.09.

## 4. COMPLETE THE SUMMONS

Use the current Minnesota Judicial Branch form CIV802, Civil Summons.

The summons must:

- identify the correct court and every party;
- be signed by Plaintiff;
- state a United States address where Plaintiff can be served personally and by mail;
- direct each Defendant to serve an Answer within the period required by the current rules;
- contain the default warning required by Minn. R. Civ. P. 4.01.

The ordinary answer period is 21 days after service under Minn. R. Civ. P. 12.01. A returned waiver request ordinarily changes the response period under Rule 4.05. Use the current CIV802 language rather than an old summons template.

## 5. COMMENCE THE ACTION BY VALID SERVICE

Minnesota ordinarily uses service commencement. Under Minn. R. Civ. P. 3.01, the action begins against each Defendant when:

- the summons is served;
- a waiver of service is signed under Rule 4.05; or
- the summons is properly delivered to the sheriff under Rule 3.01(c), followed by actual service within 60 days.

Do not personally serve the summons and complaint. Personal service ordinarily must be made by a nonparty at least 18 years old under Rule 4.02.

For a business entity, service must comply with Rule 4.03 and the entity’s actual legal form. Confirm whether service should be made on a registered agent, officer, managing agent, general partner, or another authorized recipient.

Lower-cost option:

1. Send CIV022A, Notice of Lawsuit and Request for Waiver of Service of Summons.
2. Include the summons and complaint.
3. Include two copies of CIV022B, Waiver of Service of Summons.
4. Include a prepaid return method.
5. Preserve proof of mailing.

A waiver request is not itself service. Commencement occurs when the waiver is signed.

## 6. FILE THE COURT PACKAGE

After valid commencement, file:

1. Civil Summons.
2. Complaint.
3. Proof of service or signed waiver.
4. CIV117 Civil Cover Sheet.
5. CIV102 Certificate of Representation and Parties, when required by the current filing instructions.
6. Filing fee or granted fee-waiver order.

Minn. R. Civ. P. 5.04 requires a nonfamily civil action to be filed within one year after commencement unless a written stipulation extends the deadline. Do not wait near the deadline.

The Minnesota Judicial Branch “Starting a Civil Case in District Court” packet contains CIV801 instructions, CIV802 summons, CIV803 complaint, CIV117 cover sheet, and SOP102 affidavit of personal service.

## 7. FEE WAIVER

When payment is not possible, complete the civil filing documents first and then submit:

- FEE102 Affidavit to Request Fee Waiver;
- FEE103 Supplemental Affidavit when required;
- proof of income, public assistance, or inability to pay;
- the summons, complaint, Civil Cover Sheet, and other documents the county requires.

The court decides whether all or part of the filing fee and specified service costs are waived. A fee waiver does not eliminate every possible litigation cost or later judgment.

## 8. DO NOT FILE SENSITIVE IDENTIFIERS

Do not place these in the public complaint or exhibits:

- Social Security number;
- full birth date;
- full bank or payment-card number;
- medical-record account identifiers not necessary to the claim;
- another person’s private identifying information.

Minn. R. Civ. P. 11.02 and Minnesota General Rules of Practice Rule 11 govern restricted identifiers. Redact public exhibits and use the court’s confidential-information process where required.

## 9. EVIDENCE PACKAGE

Create a separate, indexed evidence file before service:

### A. Transaction chronology

For each procedure list:

- date;
- company and facility;
- arrival and departure times;
- advertised payment;
- actual payment;
- extraction volume shown;
- weight category;
- staff statements;
- adverse event or deferral;
- laboratory results;
- transportation expense.

### B. Representation matrix

For each challenged statement list:

- exact quote or faithful paraphrase;
- screenshot or document filename;
- date captured;
- audience;
- why it is alleged to be false or incomplete;
- information supporting that allegation;
- decision made in reliance;
- resulting injury.

### C. Consent comparison

Obtain every version of the consent documents used during Plaintiff’s participation. Compare:

- disclosed procedure steps;
- disclosed immediate risks;
- disclosed cumulative risks;
- frequency disclosures;
- longitudinal monitoring statements;
- extraction-volume statements;
- compensation disclosures;
- commercial-use disclosures;
- record-access provisions.

### D. Public-benefit evidence

Preserve evidence that the same representations are directed to the public at large:

- public websites;
- mobile-app screens;
- mass advertising;
- facility signage;
- standardized videos;
- standardized consent documents;
- referral programs;
- standardized bonus schedules.

This evidence is central to the public-benefit requirement under Ly v. Nystrom and Collins v. Minnesota School of Business.

## 10. REQUEST RECORDS BEFORE OR WITH FILING

Send each Defendant a written request for the complete participant record, including:

- enrollment and consent documents;
- procedure dates;
- extraction volumes;
- device identifiers;
- pre- and post-procedure observations;
- laboratory results;
- deferrals;
- adverse-event records;
- communications;
- payment and bonus history;
- record of consent-form versions presented.

Send a separate preservation notice identifying the categories of evidence pleaded in the complaint. A preservation notice does not create discovery power before commencement, but it documents notice of foreseeable litigation.

## 11. MEDICAL-CAUSATION AND PROFESSIONAL-NEGLIGENCE GATE

The present complaint intentionally does not plead a Minnesota medical-malpractice or professional-negligence count.

Do not add such a count without determining whether Minn. Stat. § 145.682 requires expert-review and expert-identification affidavits. Failure to satisfy that statute can result in mandatory dismissal of claims requiring expert testimony.

A qualified clinician should review:

- complete procedure history;
- laboratory trends;
- deferrals;
- claimed symptoms or diagnoses;
- alternative causes;
- whether additional monitoring was medically indicated;
- whether any claimed injury was caused by a particular Defendant.

## 12. FEDERAL-LAW PLEADING GATE

Do not plead that violation of an FDA regulation automatically creates a private damages claim. The complaint uses federal law as regulatory context and evidence relevant to materiality and reasonableness.

The state-law theory must remain independent:

- false or misleading Minnesota advertising;
- material omissions in Minnesota commercial transactions;
- deceptive standardized consent presentation;
- concrete reliance and injury;
- prospective deceptive-practice relief.

Do not convert the action into a claim that exists only because the federal government allegedly failed to enforce federal law.

## 13. DAMAGES AND STRUCTURAL RELIEF

Do not insert a billion-dollar figure unless admissible evidence and a legally valid damages method support it.

The complaint presently requests:

- Plaintiff’s provable compensatory loss;
- legally available restitution;
- prospective injunctions;
- declarations;
- preservation and targeted accounting;
- costs and disbursements allowed by law.

No judgment, settlement proceeds, or $1.2 billion fund presently exists. No transfer to Caeluviim can be represented as completed. A future allocation proposal can be documented only as contingent governance planning after lawful recovery and subject to the court, settlement terms, tax obligations, liens, claimant rights, and counsel obligations.

## 14. PRE-SERVICE RULE 11 REVIEW

Read every numbered paragraph aloud and classify it:

- personal knowledge;
- authenticated document;
- reasonable inference;
- information and belief expected to obtain support in discovery;
- unsupported.

Delete every unsupported allegation. Attach no exhibit merely because it is rhetorically powerful. The signed complaint certifies that the filing is not for an improper purpose, legal contentions are warranted or nonfrivolous, and factual contentions have or are likely to obtain evidentiary support.

## 15. EXPECTED DEFENSES

Prepare for motions asserting:

- failure to plead fraud with enough particularity;
- no concrete injury or causal nexus;
- no public benefit;
- federal preemption or impermissible private FDA enforcement;
- consent or assumption of risk;
- statute of limitations;
- improper venue;
- wrong entity or defective service;
- failure to satisfy expert-affidavit requirements;
- speculative damages;
- lack of standing for industry-wide relief.

The best response is a complaint tied to exact statements, dates, records, reliance decisions, concrete loss, and ongoing public-facing conduct.
