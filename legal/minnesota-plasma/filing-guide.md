# Minnesota Commercial Plasmapheresis Filing Guide

> **FILING-FORM PACKAGE - SERVICE HOLD UNTIL ALL MANDATORY FIELDS ARE VERIFIED**

## 1. Operational status

The complaint is structured in filing form, but it is not ready to sign or serve while any bracketed field remains. Missing facts may not be invented. The mandatory items include the plaintiff's current legal name and service address; exact county and venue basis; exact defendant legal entities and registered agents; exact transaction dates and locations; exact words relied upon; reliance and concrete injury; and every supporting exhibit.

## 2. Source-supported core

The source pleading supports a consumer-protection theory based on Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a; standardized donation terminology; compensated transactions; direct participation at CSL Plasma and BioLife Plasma Services; a CSL bandage, supervisory-review, security, and exclusion incident; BioLife compensation amounts requiring record verification; and broader allegations pleaded on information and belief.

The following material is deliberately omitted from the filing form at the initial stage: duplicated or reserved counts; class or representative language; unsupported corporate-lineage or conspiracy allegations; unverified epidemiological calculations; drafting notes; unresolved nonfactual placeholders; a structural relief pool; a specific billion-dollar demand; and non-pleading philosophical material.

## 4. Claim architecture

**Count I - Minnesota Consumer Fraud Act.** The count alleges that the overall participant-facing presentation used a material misrepresentation, misleading statement, deceptive practice, or unfair practice in a compensated exchange involving services and intangibles. A threshold issue is whether the exchange qualifies as a consumer sale of merchandise under Minn. Stat. § 325F.68. The complaint pleads services, consideration, and consumer use directly. The claim also requires proof of wrongful conduct, actual injury, causal nexus, and public benefit.

**Count II - False Statement in Advertising.** The count requires an actual advertisement identified by date, medium, exact words, source or location, defendant, and exhibit. It must plead why the statement was materially deceptive, how Plaintiff encountered it and acted in response, and the resulting injury and causal nexus.

**Count III - Declaratory and equitable relief.** The count is limited to an actual controversy concerning specific proved representations and omissions. It requires a truthful present basis for ongoing or realistically threatened injury. Delete Count III and the prospective-relief requests when that basis is absent.

## 5. Pre-service verification procedure

1. Replace every bracketed field. Search the completed complaint and summonses for `[` and `]`; a zero-result search is the minimum completion test.
2. Verify each defendant through Minnesota Secretary of State Business Filings Online on the same day service is arranged. Preserve the current entity record, filing number, status, and registered agent or authorized office.
3. Create one evidence folder per defendant. Preserve original files and create dated working copies. Record acquisition date, source, and file hash where practical.
4. Save dated PDF or screenshot copies of each public statement relied upon. Web pages can change.
5. Match every exact quotation to an exhibit or contemporaneous record. Do not paraphrase inside quotation marks.
6. Confirm venue under Minn. Stat. § 542.09. Hennepin County is not automatic merely because the Fourth Judicial District is preferred.
7. Run a Rule 11 audit. Each factual contention must have existing evidentiary support or be specifically identified as likely to receive support after reasonable discovery.
8. Use identical party names and county across the complaint, each summons, civil cover sheet, exhibit list, service documents, and filing data.

## 6. Commencement and service sequence

1. Prepare one completed summons for each defendant and one identical signed complaint.
2. Choose personal service or request waiver of service. Mailing a summons alone does not complete service. A waiver is effective only when the defendant signs and returns it.
3. For personal service, use a sheriff, private process server, or another adult who is at least 18 and is not a party. Plaintiff may not personally serve the papers.
4. Serve the registered agent, officer, managing agent, or other authorized agent in the manner allowed by Minn. R. Civ. P. 4.03. Use the verified legal entity and exact service address.
5. Obtain an affidavit or certificate stating time, place, and manner of service. Keep the original.
6. The action is commenced against each defendant when service occurs, a waiver is signed, or another method authorized by Rule 3.01 applies.
7. After personal service, the ordinary answer period is generally 21 days. A defendant returning a waiver ordinarily receives the period stated in Rule 4.05.
8. Rule 5.04 requires filing within one year after commencement unless a timely stipulation extends the period. File the case and proof of service promptly rather than relying on the outer deadline.

## 9. Medical-claim boundary

The filing form does not allege malpractice, professional-negligence, medical-error, product-liability, or mistake theories. Minnesota medical-malpractice claims may invoke expert-review affidavit and limitation requirements. Scientific or technical allegations concerning infection incidence, testing, pooling, device alarms, extraction frequency, or causal injury require qualified individual expert analysis and appropriate evidentiary support. They remain excluded from the initial complaint except for noncontroversial procedure description and narrowly stated firsthand observation.

## 10. Damages, assignment, and Caeluviim

The complaint requests reasonable unliquidated damages greater than $50,000 rather than a speculative multi-billion-dollar sum, consistent with Minn. R. Civ. P. 8.01. No recovery exists unless obtained by judgment or lawful settlement. No present lawsuit recovery or transfer to Caeluviim exists or can be recorded as an existing asset. Any future assignment, donation, trust, or organizational transfer requires an actual recovery, lawful authority, and separate legal and tax review.
