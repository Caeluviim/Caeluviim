## Factual foundation pleaded

### Recurring commercial transaction

The pleading alleges a compensated recurring transaction involving screening, blood sampling, venous access, automated extracorporeal processing, plasma retention, return of cellular components, replacement fluid, and post-session compensation, while participant-facing communications use donation, donor, stick, generosity, and life-saving language.

Plaintiff alleges that the combined presentation becomes materially misleading when it obscures commercial purpose, recurrence, invasiveness, compensation conditions, specialized commercial use, protein-related deferral meaning, collection-volume information, or safety-alert response.

### BioLife

The pleading alleges repeated compensated participation during 2025 and 2026, including the Sun Ray Shopping Center operation; compensation of approximately $45 for a first weekly session and $75 for a second weekly session during at least part of 2026; approximately four to six hours per week committed to sessions and travel; approximately $8 weekly transit expense; and approximately $120 weekly compensation under at least one schedule.

Plaintiff recalls weighing approximately 150 pounds or less and machine-displayed collection targets or volumes commonly in the range of approximately 820 to 830 milliliters. The Complaint does not declare those targets unlawful; it seeks the target-setting rule, machine protocol, anticoagulant amount, and individualized records.

The pleading identifies a larger-container statement substantially as follows: `Just because it can hold more does not necessarily mean they are going to take more.` It also alleges one 2025 or 2026 session in which the device stopped or generated a safety alert three times, successive personnel allowed continuation, and a nurse stated in substance that intervention was not called until six occurrences in one session. The Complaint expressly does not label those alerts as hemolysis without machine records or qualified evidence.

### Grifols / Talecris

The pleading alleges a low-protein clinical deferral lasting more than one year at the Saint Paul operation while Plaintiff was accepted for some BioLife sessions and later experienced additional protein-related deferrals. It challenges materially incomplete dietary framing and seeks protein, immunoglobulin, physician-review, deferral, and reinstatement records.

### Octapharma

The pleading alleges a 2026 two-vial blood-sample collection using a large-bore needle that staff identified as 17 gauge, a statement that the center stocked only that needle type for the collection, repeated blood samples or laboratory panels during re-entry or clearance, and a later clinical deferral. It does not allege that every additional sample is automatically unlawful. It challenges nondisclosure of purpose, scope, frequency, and available alternatives.

### CSL

The pleading alleges a tetanus immunization through a two-session process at the Saint Paul operation, approximately $5 additional compensation, consent to receive the vaccine, and failure clearly to disclose that the immunization was connected to inducing elevated tetanus-antibody concentrations for a commercially distinct high-titer or hyperimmune plasma program. It seeks the responsible-physician approval, immunization protocol, consent materials, vaccine record, product designation, specialized compensation, and disposition records.

## Relief requested

The Complaint seeks compensatory and consequential damages proved at trial; recoverable costs, disbursements, investigation costs, and attorney fees if incurred; restitution limited to benefits unjustly obtained from Plaintiff; declaratory relief; narrowly tailored prospective correction or discontinuance of proven materially misleading participant-facing communications; interest; amendment when supported by discovery; and other lawful relief.
