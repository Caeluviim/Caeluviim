# Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a — Standing, Counts, Remedy, and Missing Instruction

Date: 2026-08-03
Status: Correction to the current ready-to-sign package

## Current draft summary

The current Complaint is an individual Ramsey County consumer-protection action against CSL Plasma Inc., Talecris Plasma Resources, Inc. doing business as Grifols Plasma, Octapharma Plasma, Inc., and BioLife Plasma Services, L.P.

It alleges that standardized donation, safety, compensation, screening, deferral, collection-volume, and special-immunization communications materially obscured the commercial and procedural character of compensated plasma extraction. The pleading relies on Plaintiff's BioLife compensation and safety-alert experience, Grifols low-protein deferral, Octapharma sampling and deferral experience, and CSL tetanus/high-titer program experience.

## Existing counts

1. Minnesota Consumer Fraud Act, Minn. Stat. § 325F.69, through Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a.
2. False Statement in Advertising, Minn. Stat. § 325F.67, through Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a.
3. Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act, Minn. Stat. §§ 325D.44-.45, for prospective injunctive relief.
4. Unjust enrichment, pleaded in the alternative and limited to benefits obtained from Plaintiff.
5. Declaratory judgment under Minn. Stat. ch. 555 and Minn. R. Civ. P. 57.

## Existing requested remedy

The current prayer requests:

- compensatory and consequential damages proved at trial;
- costs, disbursements, investigation costs, and reasonable attorney fees if incurred and recoverable;
- restitution limited to benefits unjustly obtained from Plaintiff;
- declarations concerning proven statutory violations;
- narrowly tailored prospective correction or discontinuance of specific misleading participant-facing communications;
- prejudgment and postjudgment interest;
- leave to amend when discovery provides support; and
- other lawful relief.

It does not request Attorney-General-only civil penalties, an administrator, absent-person damages, a structural relief pool, class relief, punitive damages, or a billion-dollar award.

## The missing § 8.31 instruction

The current draft does not implement the stated theory of enforcing a Minnesota statute that lacks its own independent private right of action. It pleads only conventional consumer-protection statutes and expressly treats federal regulations as evidentiary benchmarks rather than causes of action.

The controlling drafting instruction is:

> For every Minnesota statute that does not independently authorize a private action, create a separate count titled “Violation of [exact Minnesota statute], enforced through Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a.” The count must allege: (1) the exact affirmative duty imposed by the Minnesota statute; (2) the exact conduct violating that duty; (3) why that statute regulates an unfair, discriminatory, or other unlawful practice in business, commerce, or trade under Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 1; (4) Plaintiff's personal injury and causal nexus; (5) the systematic or public-facing character producing public benefit; and (6) the private remedies sought under subdivision 3a. Do not request Attorney-General-only powers. Do not use subdivision 3a to convert federal law into a state private cause of action. Do not plead the count when the underlying statute expressly excludes a subdivision 3a action.

This is the Findling framework. Findling v. Group Health Plan, Inc., 998 N.W.2d 1 (Minn. 2023), holds that the set of laws privately enforceable through subdivision 3a aligns with the set of Minnesota laws the Attorney General may investigate and enforce under subdivision 1. The underlying statute need not independently provide a private cause of action, but it must be a law of Minnesota respecting unfair, discriminatory, or other unlawful practices in business, commerce, or trade. The plaintiff must still plead injury and public benefit.

## Caption and capacity correction

“Statutory enforcement relator” may be retained as a project descriptor, but the operative court status should be stated as an injured private plaintiff proceeding under Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a. A private subdivision 3a plaintiff sues on the plaintiff's own behalf and does not possess the Attorney General's representative authority, civil-penalty power, pre-suit investigative powers, assurance-of-discontinuance authority, or power to bind the State.

Recommended caption language:

> YŪREI, also known as BRIAN THOMAS JOHNSON, individually and as an injured private plaintiff proceeding under Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a.

Recommended operative allegation:

> Plaintiff invokes Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a as the supplemental private cause of action and remedial vehicle for each specifically identified Minnesota law pleaded below. For every unenumerated statute, Plaintiff alleges that the statute regulates an unfair or other unlawful practice in business, commerce, or trade within Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 1; that Defendant violated the statute's specific duty; that Plaintiff was injured by the violation; and that the challenged practice is systematic or public-facing and therefore benefits the public when corrected. Plaintiff seeks only the private remedies authorized by subdivision 3a and does not exercise or request powers reserved to the Attorney General.

## Procedural clarification

Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a does not itself require a separate court form, Attorney General permission, or pre-suit notice to the Attorney General. The required instruction is a pleading architecture requirement inside the Complaint: exact underlying Minnesota law, exact duty, exact violation, business-or-commerce nexus, personal injury and causation, public benefit, and subdivision 3a remedy.

## Finality determination

The current Complaint accurately summarizes the conventional counts and available private remedies, but it is not final relative to the user's stated novel enforcement theory because it contains no Findling-style count based on a separately identified Minnesota regulatory statute lacking an independent private remedy. No such count should be added until the exact Minnesota statute, duty, violation, injury, and public-benefit allegations are identified and verified.
