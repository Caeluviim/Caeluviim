## Source-boundary rule

This record preserves a correction to the causal model. It distinguishes institutional assembly and retention of persons from community formation among those persons. It does not represent the institution's purpose, funding motive, service metrics, or public representations as verified facts. Those program-specific propositions remain pending evidence and validation.

## Corrected causal distinction

### OBS-COM-001 — Institutional assembly is not community creation

The reporting participant states that material inducements, controlled access, recurring programming, and other attendance mechanisms do not create the community that becomes visible in the shared space. Those mechanisms assemble, attract, retain, and consolidate persons in a location for institutional purposes that may include service capture, reporting, referral, behavioral management, target consolidation, or monetization.

Community manifests separately because persons occupy a space in common. Repeated co-presence creates the conditions for recognition, familiarity, interpersonal relation, mutual awareness, informal norms, and other social formations. That community is generated through the persons' shared occupancy and relations, not through the institutional purpose for which they were assembled.

Status: first-person conceptual correction and analytical articulation.

## Analytical claims for validation

### CLM-COM-001 — Assembly mechanism and community formation are causally distinct

Institutional inducements and access controls may produce the assembly and retention of persons without producing the community that later manifests among them. The assembly mechanism is an institutional cause of co-location. Community formation is a separate social consequence of human co-presence and relation.

Epistemic status: analytical distinction.
Validation required for program-specific application: operating rules, inducement schedules, access restrictions, attendance patterns, participant accounts, spatial-use observations, and organizational descriptions of community.

### CLM-COM-002 — Instrumental target consolidation

Material benefits, restricted access windows, recurring events, and attendance-conditioned incentives may function as mechanisms for attracting, retaining, and consolidating an administratively legible target population. The institution's operational purpose for that concentration may differ from the social purposes and relations developed by the persons occupying the space.

Epistemic status: contested institutional hypothesis.
Validation required: program budgets, event rules, attendance conditions, service-output definitions, grant metrics, participant-flow records, and internal planning materials.

### CLM-COM-003 — Emergent community belongs causally to the persons

Where community manifests through repeated shared occupancy, its causal source is the persons' co-presence, recognition, interaction, and relation. It should not be attributed to the institution merely because the institution controls the site or created the conditions under which persons were assembled.

Epistemic status: social and causal proposition.
Boundary: the institution may provide a necessary physical condition without being the author, owner, or governing cause of the community.

### CLM-COM-004 — Institutional appropriation of emergent social value

An institution may publicly present the resulting familiarity, solidarity, or appearance of community as evidence that the institution created, supports, or embodies community, even where its own mechanisms were directed principally toward assembling and processing a target population for other operational purposes.

Epistemic status: contested public-framing hypothesis.
Validation required: public communications, grant narratives, fundraising materials, program reports, internal objectives, and participant accounts.

### CLM-COM-005 — Community does not legitimate the assembly apparatus

The presence of an emergent community does not validate the institutional purposes, inducements, restrictions, funding structures, or service-conversion mechanisms that assembled the population. Social value generated by participants cannot be used without further analysis as evidence that the underlying apparatus is beneficial, noncoercive, or community-directed.

Epistemic status: normative and institutional assessment.

## Causal model

Institutional layer:

`material inducements + controlled access + recurring programming + service eligibility -> assembly and retention of persons`

Social layer:

`repeated co-presence in shared space -> recognition + relation + emergent community`

Appropriation risk:

`institutionally assembled population + participant-generated community -> institutional claim of community creation or support`

The first arrow does not entail that the institution created the community. The second arrow identifies the community's proximate social cause. The third identifies a potential public-framing operation requiring evidence.

## Evidence acquisition plan

1. Inventory every material inducement, access restriction, time condition, attendance condition, and recurring program associated with the site.
2. Determine which benefits require presence, duration, check-in, testing, referral, enrollment, or other qualifying conduct.
3. Obtain event schedules, written rules, staff scripts, budgets, grant applications, performance reports, and attendance metrics.
4. Separate institutional service contacts from participant-to-participant interactions and community activity.
5. Obtain participant accounts concerning why they attend, why they remain, and what relationships or social practices arise independently of staff direction.
6. Compare public claims of community creation or support with internal objectives, funded deliverables, and actual governance of the shared space.
7. Determine whether participant-generated social value is used in grant, fundraising, public-relations, or program-legitimacy narratives.
8. Preserve distinctions among providing physical space, assembling a target population, governing a program, and generating community.
