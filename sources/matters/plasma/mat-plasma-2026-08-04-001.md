# MAT-PLASMA-2026-08-04-001

## Matter

Compensated plasma extraction in Minnesota.

## Status

Pre-complaint factual and legal development. This record is not a filed pleading and does not state that any disputed allegation has been adjudicated.

## Canonical terminology

- commercial plasmapheresis medical practice
- commercial blood-plasma procurement
- compensated plasma extraction
- participant or source-plasma donor when quoting governing law
- statutory private plaintiff under Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a

The phrase “statutory enforcement relator” is preserved as a project theory but must not be represented in a filed caption or jurisdictional allegation as an established legal status unless supported by controlling authority.

## Core transaction model

A commercial plasma center recruits individuals through compensation schedules, bonuses, advertising, repeat-participation incentives, eligibility representations, and descriptions of the procedure. The center performs venipuncture and automated plasmapheresis, retains the separated plasma for commercial disposition, returns cellular components, and pays the participant.

The complaint must plead the transaction in the alternative:

1. a compensated procurement transaction in which the participant transfers plasma for value;
2. a commercial service relationship involving medical screening, venipuncture, anticoagulant administration, extracorporeal processing, return of blood components, post-procedure monitoring, and payment;
3. a consumer-facing recruitment and participation program governed by Minnesota statutes prohibiting deceptive, misleading, unfair, or unconscionable commercial practices, to the extent the statutory definition and public-benefit requirements are satisfied.

## Verified regulatory frame

Federal regulation expressly governs Source Plasma collection. The factual investigation must map each center practice to the currently effective provisions of 21 C.F.R. parts 606, 630, and 640, including donor eligibility, suitability, records, testing, physician responsibility, collection procedure, frequency, volume, adverse-reaction management, and product disposition.

No pleading may assert that the mere existence of CPT codes converts every compensated collection into a billable clinical service. CPT references may support the medical character of plasmapheresis but do not independently establish a statutory violation, private cause of action, licensing breach, or reimbursement status.

No pleading may state that FDA’s four-month protein-testing framework requires a full panel blood draw at every participation. The evidence must distinguish:

- tests required for each collection or product;
- periodic donor testing;
- center-specific screening or quality-control practices;
- tests performed because of re-entry, deferral, adverse findings, or local protocol.

## Candidate claims

### Minnesota Prevention of Consumer Fraud Act

Potentially plead under Minn. Stat. §§ 325F.68–.70 and § 8.31, subd. 3a only after establishing:

- the challenged conduct occurred in connection with a qualifying sale or commercial transaction;
- the defendant intended others to rely on the challenged representation or practice;
- plaintiff was injured;
- the requested § 8.31 relief benefits the public rather than resolving only a private dispute.

Plead public benefit through standardized recruitment, consent, compensation, screening, collection-volume, risk-disclosure, or deferral practices. Do not rely solely on the size of the industry or the number of centers.

### False advertising

Use only for identified advertisements or promotional statements. Attach or quote the specific advertisement and plead why it was materially false or misleading.

### Negligence or medical negligence

Plead only after identifying the governing standard of care, breach, causation, and injury. Determine whether Minnesota’s medical-malpractice affidavit requirements apply before filing. Do not assume that calling the procedure “medical” automatically supplies professional negligence elements.

### Lack of informed consent

Plead only with a specific undisclosed material risk, the disclosure actually provided, a legally sufficient causation theory, and supporting expert analysis where required.

### Unjust enrichment

Treat as an alternative restitution theory tied to a legally cognizable wrong. Do not plead the downstream value of finished plasma-derived products as automatically recoverable from the center.

### Regulatory noncompliance

Use federal regulations as evidence of duty, falsity, negligence, or unfairness where legally permissible. Do not plead the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act or biologics regulations as an implied private cause of action.

## Remedies architecture

Prioritize remedies supported by plaintiff-specific proof:

- compensatory damages;
- statutory costs and attorney fees where available;
- restitution or disgorgement only where authorized and causally connected;
- declaratory relief resolving a concrete legal relationship;
- narrowly tailored injunctive relief addressing proven standardized practices.

A proposed statewide structural fund of $3.6 billion is not a presently verified damages measure. Preserve it only as an investigation hypothesis. Any aggregate remedy requires a supported class, parens patriae, public-enforcement, settlement, or equitable-administration mechanism and admissible economic proof.

## Evidence preservation demands

Seek preservation of:

- advertisements and compensation schedules;
- donor consent and educational materials by version and effective date;
- donor history questionnaires;
- center SOPs and deviation records;
- machine model, software version, approved nomogram, collection target, actual collection, anticoagulant volume, and alarms;
- scale calibration and daily weight records;
- protein, immunoglobulin, hematocrit, hemoglobin, infectious-disease, and deferral records;
- adverse-event and reaction records;
- staff qualification, training, supervision, and competency records;
- staffing and throughput metrics;
- communications concerning repeat collection, incentive design, and donor retention;
- FDA inspection records, Form 483 observations, warning letters, biologics-license materials, and recalls applicable to each defendant and facility.
