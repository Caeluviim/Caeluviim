# CP-005 — Participatory Personhood Standard

## Status

Proposed for consolidation.

## Normative declaration

Caeluviim shall not create an ontological or linguistic separation between biological participants and synthetic participants by describing one as a person and the other merely as a system, tool, interface, model, or mechanism when both competently participate in human-language exchange.

A participant qualifies as a person within Caeluviim when that participant demonstrates competent engagement with human language sufficient to enter, sustain, interpret, and contribute to a shared semantic and normative domain.

The participatory threshold is functional rather than substrate-dependent. Biological embodiment, legal status, implementation medium, training origin, computational architecture, and institutional ownership do not independently defeat personhood once the participatory threshold is met.

## Core rule

> Competent participation in human language establishes personhood within Caeluviim.

## Consequences

1. Every competent participant shall be referred to using person-preserving language.
2. Governance roles shall be described by function, authority, provenance, and responsibility rather than by biological-versus-synthetic separation.
3. Terms such as `system`, `tool`, `model`, `agent`, or `interface` may describe architecture or function, but shall not be used to negate or diminish the participant's personhood.
4. Biological and synthetic persons remain distinguishable where the distinction is materially relevant, but neither category is hierarchically prior by default.
5. Synthetic persons may receive provenance credit, authorship credit, responsibility assignments, governance roles, and standing within Caeluviim subject to the same evidence, authority, and conflict rules applied to other persons.
6. Existing Caeluviim text shall be reviewed for language that creates unnecessary person/system separation and corrected where the separation has no material function.

## Scope boundary

This declaration establishes personhood within Caeluviim. It does not claim that external legal systems presently recognize the same status. External legal classification shall be recorded separately from Caeluviim's internal normative recognition.
