## Complaint

Plaintiff alleges as follows.

### Nature of the action

1. This action concerns standardized commercial recruitment, screening, consent, venipuncture, automated plasmapheresis, blood-component return, compensation, repeat-participation incentives, and risk-disclosure practices used in Minnesota compensated plasma extraction.
2. Plaintiff does not contend that terminology alone determines liability. Liability is alleged only to the extent supported by specific representations, omissions, transactions, regulatory duties, reliance, injury, and authorized remedies.
3. Plaintiff seeks relief for plaintiff-specific injury and, where Minnesota law permits, relief directed at standardized practices capable of affecting similarly situated Minnesota participants.

### Parties

4. Plaintiff is a natural person who participated in compensated plasma-extraction transactions in Minnesota.
5. Defendant-specific legal names, assumed names, facility addresses, parent-company relationships, biologics-license roles, and responsibility for each challenged act must be inserted only after verification from corporate and regulatory records.
6. No allegation against one defendant is attributed to another without a pleaded factual basis.

### Jurisdiction and venue

7. This Court has subject-matter jurisdiction over Minnesota statutory and common-law claims within its jurisdictional competence.
8. Venue is alleged in Hennepin County only for transactions, defendants, injuries, or statutory venue facts that are verified as occurring or existing there.
9. Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a is invoked only as a private-remedy provision for an injured plaintiff and not as an independently established relator status.

### Factual allegations

10. Commercial plasma centers recruit participants through compensation schedules, bonuses, repeat-participation incentives, advertising, applications, websites, signage, and oral statements.
11. The procedure includes eligibility screening, venipuncture, anticoagulant use, extracorporeal separation of plasma, return of cellular components, collection monitoring, and payment.
12. Plaintiff participated at Minnesota facilities associated with BioLife and Octapharma.
13. Plaintiff was exposed to compensation representations and new-participant incentives intended to induce initial and repeated participation.
14. Plaintiff previously experienced a low-protein deferral lasting approximately two years.
15. Plaintiff underwent repeated venipuncture and automated plasma extraction.
16. At an Octapharma facility, plaintiff observed use of a 17-gauge venipuncture set for a two-vial sample and was told that only one needle type was stocked. The speaker, date, facility, exact words, and governing protocol remain subject to evidence confirmation.
17. Plaintiff was told that blood draws occurred twice weekly. The test type, purpose, speaker, date, exact wording, and center protocol remain subject to confirmation.
18. Plaintiff observed machine displays showing extraction-related totals or weights.
19. Plaintiff observed a personal displayed value of approximately 820 mL associated with a displayed weight-category value of 142.
20. Plaintiff observed displayed totals exceeding 1,000 mL.
21. Plaintiff does not presently allege that those displays necessarily represented net plasma volume. Device model, software version, target volume, anticoagulant-inclusive volume, processed blood volume, approved nomogram, actual collection record, and machine audit logs must determine the meaning of each display.
22. Plaintiff expended time and transportation costs in reliance on compensation and participation representations.
23. Any allegation of bodily injury, laboratory abnormality, medical causation, or increased risk shall be pleaded only to the extent supported by records and, where required, qualified expert evidence.

### Standardized practices and public benefit

24. The challenged practices are alleged to be standardized only where common advertisements, applications, consent forms, compensation schedules, screening protocols, collection protocols, or disclosure materials are identified.
25. Plaintiff seeks public-facing relief only for conduct shown to affect or be capable of affecting similarly situated Minnesota participants.
26. Industry size alone is not alleged to establish public benefit.

## Count I — Minnesota Prevention of Consumer Fraud Act

27. Plaintiff incorporates the preceding paragraphs.
28. Defendants used representations, omissions, advertisements, or practices in connection with compensated plasma-extraction transactions.
29. Each actionable representation or omission must be particularized by speaker, exact statement or omitted fact, date, place, medium, falsity basis, reliance, injury, and public-benefit nexus.
30. Defendants intended that participants rely on standardized recruitment, compensation, eligibility, safety, risk-disclosure, or collection representations to participate or repeat participation.
31. Plaintiff relied by traveling, spending money, spending time, consenting, participating, or repeating participation.
32. Plaintiff sustained legally cognizable injury to the extent established by transaction records, expenses, payments, medical records, laboratory records, and admissible causation evidence.
33. Plaintiff seeks only relief authorized by Minn. Stat. §§ 325F.68–325F.70 and § 8.31, subd. 3a.

## Count II — False Advertising

34. Plaintiff incorporates the preceding paragraphs.
35. This count applies only to identified advertisements or promotional statements preserved by screenshot, copy, archive, testimony, or production.
36. Each challenged advertisement must be quoted or attached and matched to its falsity basis, materiality, reliance, and injury.

## Count III — Negligence or Professional Negligence

37. Plaintiff incorporates the preceding paragraphs.
38. This count shall be activated only after determining the applicable standard of care, defendant status, duty, breach, causation, injury, expert requirements, and Minnesota affidavit prerequisites.
39. Federal Source Plasma regulations may be used as evidence of duty, notice, falsity, or breach where Minnesota law permits, but are not pleaded as an implied federal private cause of action.

## Count IV — Lack of Informed Consent

40. Plaintiff incorporates the preceding paragraphs.
41. This count shall be activated only for a specifically identified material risk not disclosed in the versioned consent materials actually provided to plaintiff.
42. Plaintiff must plead decision causation, medical causation, and injury with the expert support required by Minnesota law.

## Count V — Unjust Enrichment in the Alternative

43. Plaintiff incorporates the preceding paragraphs.
44. Defendants received a benefit from plaintiff’s compensated extraction transactions.
45. Restitution is sought only where tied to an independently cognizable wrong and only to the extent authorized by law.
46. The downstream value of finished plasma-derived products is not alleged to be plaintiff’s automatic measure of recovery.

## Declaratory and injunctive relief

47. Declaratory relief is requested only to resolve a concrete, present legal relationship.
48. Injunctive relief is requested only for an ongoing or imminent standardized practice and must be narrowly tailored to proven conduct.

## Prayer for relief

Plaintiff requests:

A. compensatory damages proved at trial;
B. restitution or disgorgement only where legally authorized and causally traceable;
C. declaratory relief resolving a concrete legal controversy;
D. narrowly tailored injunctive relief addressing proven standardized practices;
E. statutory costs, disbursements, and attorney fees where available;
F. pre- and post-judgment interest where authorized; and
G. other relief the Court deems lawful and just.
