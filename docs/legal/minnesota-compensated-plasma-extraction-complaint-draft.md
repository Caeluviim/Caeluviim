## Introduction

1. This action concerns a commercial plasmapheresis system in which defendants procure human blood plasma through repeated compensated extraction procedures while representing the transaction through the language and operational framing of “donation.”
2. Plaintiff does not rely on terminology alone. The claims arise from specific representations, omissions, compensation practices, screening and testing practices, procedure-level conduct, and the resulting economic and physical injuries.
3. Plaintiff seeks damages and equitable relief under Minnesota law for actionable deceptive or unfair practices that injured plaintiff and that were presented to, and capable of affecting, members of the public.
4. Federal blood and biologics regulations are pleaded as relevant standards, context, notice, and evidence of falsity or unfairness where state law permits. They are not pleaded as an implied standalone federal private cause of action.

## Parties

5. Plaintiff is a natural person who participated in compensated plasma-extraction transactions in Minnesota.
6. Each defendant must be pleaded separately by exact legal name, registered address, role, facility relationship, and conduct attributable to that entity.
7. No defendant may be grouped with another unless the complaint states the factual basis for joint action, agency, control, alter-ego liability, successor liability, or direct participation.

## Jurisdiction and Venue

8. This Court has subject-matter jurisdiction over Minnesota statutory and common-law claims.
9. Venue is proper in Hennepin County only for transactions, defendants, injuries, or statutory venue facts connected to Hennepin County. Events occurring solely in another county must be evaluated separately before filing.
10. Plaintiff invokes Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a only as an injured person asserting a qualifying violation of a law referenced in § 8.31, subd. 1. Plaintiff does not claim governmental or attorney-general status.

## Factual Allegations

### Commercial extraction transactions

11. Defendants operate compensated plasma-procurement facilities that recruit members of the public through payment schedules, new-participant bonuses, repeat-visit incentives, app-based offers, and promotional representations.
12. Plaintiff participated because compensation was offered and because defendants represented that plaintiff was eligible to undergo the procedure under defendants’ screening, testing, safety, and disclosure systems.
13. Plaintiff’s transactions must be pleaded visit by visit, including date, facility, compensation advertised, compensation paid, wait time, procedure duration, forms shown, staff statements, test performed, collection result, displayed machine values, and post-procedure effects.

### Representations and omissions

14. Defendants used the word “donation” and related language in commercial communications while conditioning payment on successful extraction and repeat participation.
15. The complaint does not allege that the word “donation,” standing alone, is unlawful. It alleges that the overall commercial presentation may be deceptive when paired with material omissions or misleading statements about the nature, frequency, cumulative effects, testing, oversight, compensation conditions, or risks of the procedure.
16. Actionable statements and omissions must be stated with particularity, including speaker or document, exact or substantially exact content, date, place, audience, why the statement was false or misleading, plaintiff’s reliance, and resulting injury.
17. Plaintiff alleges that material information concerning cumulative physiological effects, longitudinal monitoring, repeated testing, procedure oversight, or compensation conditions was omitted or presented in a manner that affected plaintiff’s decision to participate.
18. Each omission theory must identify the source of a duty to disclose, the information withheld, defendants’ knowledge, materiality, reliance or transaction causation, and injury.

### Screening, testing, procedure, and oversight

19. Plaintiff observed and experienced repeated venipuncture and plasmapheresis procedures at commercial facilities.
20. Plaintiff reports that certain facilities used large-bore venipuncture equipment, repeated sample collection, automated extraction equipment, and displayed machine values during procedures.
21. Machine displays must not be characterized as plasma volume unless the device model, software version, nomogram, target value, actual collected plasma, anticoagulant contribution, processed volume, and event log establish that meaning.
22. Testing allegations must distinguish total-protein testing, hematocrit or hemoglobin screening, infectious-disease testing, immunoglobulin testing, full blood panels, sample-only visits, and any other test.
23. Plaintiff alleges that physician oversight was not visibly present at relevant facilities. The legal significance of that observation depends on the exact governing duty, delegation structure, records, standing orders, responsible physician role, and procedure performed.
24. No allegation of professional negligence shall be filed until Minnesota expert-review and affidavit requirements have been evaluated for the specific claim.

### Injury and causation

25. Plaintiff’s recoverable injuries must be pleaded by category and supported by evidence.
26. Economic injury may include unreimbursed transportation, reliance expenditures, withheld or reduced compensation, and other transaction-specific loss caused by an actionable practice.
27. Physical injury may include documented symptoms, adverse reactions, laboratory abnormalities, diagnosed conditions, treatment costs, or other medically supportable consequences causally connected to the procedure or omission.
28. Restitution is limited to money or value traceable to the challenged transaction and legally recoverable under the selected cause of action.
29. Industry revenue, downstream plasma value, or statewide market estimates are not themselves plaintiff damages.

## Count I — Minnesota Consumer Fraud Act

30. Plaintiff realleges the preceding paragraphs.
31. Minn. Stat. §§ 325F.68–325F.70 prohibit fraud, false promise, misrepresentation, misleading statement, or deceptive practice made with the intent that others rely on it in connection with the sale of merchandise.
32. Plaintiff must establish that the challenged transaction and conduct fall within the statute’s operative definitions and scope.
33. Defendants made or used one or more particularized representations, omissions, or deceptive practices identified in the transaction and representation schedules attached to the final complaint.
34. Defendants intended that prospective or current participants rely on those communications in deciding whether and how often to undergo compensated plasma extraction.
35. Plaintiff relied on, or was transactionally caused to act by, the challenged practice and suffered a legally cognizable injury.
36. The challenged conduct was directed to or capable of affecting similarly situated members of the public, supporting the public-benefit requirement applicable to a private action under Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a.
37. Plaintiff seeks damages, costs, disbursements, costs of investigation where recoverable, attorney fees where recoverable, and equitable relief authorized by law.

## Count II — False Advertising

38. Plaintiff realleges the preceding paragraphs.
39. For each advertisement, plaintiff must identify the medium, date, audience, exact claim, material omission, falsity or misleading tendency, reliance or causal connection, and injury.
40. Defendants disseminated one or more materially false or misleading commercial representations concerning compensation, eligibility, safety, testing, frequency, oversight, or procedure characteristics.
41. Plaintiff encountered and acted in response to the identified representation and suffered injury.
42. Plaintiff seeks relief authorized by the applicable Minnesota false-advertising provision and Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a, if all statutory elements are satisfied.

## Count III — Negligence or Professional Negligence

43. This count is conditional and must not be filed without claim-specific legal and expert screening.
44. Plaintiff must identify the applicable standard of care, defendant owing the duty, breach, factual and medical causation, and compensable injury.
45. Regulatory standards may be relevant evidence but do not automatically establish negligence per se or a private cause of action.
46. Any Minnesota affidavit-of-expert-review requirement must be satisfied or a recognized exception must be pleaded in good faith.

## Count IV — Lack of Informed Consent

47. This count is conditional and must not be filed without Minnesota-specific duty, expert, disclosure, and causation analysis.
48. Plaintiff must identify the material risk or alternative not disclosed, what was actually disclosed, what a reasonable person or this plaintiff would have decided with adequate disclosure, and the injury resulting from the procedure.
49. A generalized allegation that cumulative risks were omitted is insufficient without a procedure-specific duty and decision-causation showing.

## Count V — Restitution / Unjust Enrichment in the Alternative

50. Plaintiff realleges the preceding paragraphs to the extent consistent with alternative pleading.
51. Defendants received money, labor value, biological material, or another benefit through the challenged transaction.
52. Plaintiff must identify the specific benefit conferred, why retention is unjust, and why an adequate legal remedy does not foreclose equitable relief.
53. This count may not duplicate statutory damages or seek the entire downstream value of plasma without a legally supportable tracing and valuation theory.

## Public-Benefit Allegations

54. Defendants used standardized public-facing compensation and recruitment systems.
55. The challenged representations or omissions were not confined to a private dispute but were communicated through repeatable forms, advertisements, apps, scripts, policies, or facility practices.
56. Equitable relief would benefit current and prospective participants by correcting or restraining the same public-facing practice.
57. These allegations must be supported by actual materials and not merely by the existence of multiple facilities or participants.

## Prayer for Relief

Plaintiff requests judgment:

A. Awarding actual damages proved at trial;
B. Awarding costs, disbursements, costs of investigation, and attorney fees where authorized;
C. Ordering restitution or disgorgement only to the extent legally available and traceable to the pleaded transactions;
D. Declaring the rights and obligations of the parties where an actual justiciable controversy exists;
E. Enjoining specifically identified deceptive or unlawful practices upon proof of ongoing or imminent injury and all equitable prerequisites;
F. Awarding pre- and post-judgment interest as authorized;
G. Awarding reasonable damages in an amount greater than $50,000 if supported, rather than pleading an unsupported specific unliquidated sum;
H. Granting other relief the Court deems lawful and just.
