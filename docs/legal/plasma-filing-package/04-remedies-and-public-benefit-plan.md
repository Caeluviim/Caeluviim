# Remedies and Public-Benefit Plan

Date: 2026-08-05
Matter: MAT-PLASMA-2026-08-04-001

## Public-benefit theory

The public-benefit allegation must be proved through concrete, standardized, continuing conduct rather than labels. The operative showing is:

1. Defendants communicate materially similar representations through public advertising, applications, enrollment materials, consent forms, facility scripts, testing explanations, deferral systems, and complaint procedures.
2. Those communications reach existing and prospective Minnesota participants beyond Plaintiff.
3. The challenged practices remain capable of repetition.
4. Corrective relief changes future public-facing transactions and participant access to material information.
5. Plaintiff's damages claim is separated from the prospective public correction.

## Remedy-to-conduct matrix

| Challenged conduct | Corrective mechanism | Required proof | Limitation control |
|---|---|---|---|
| compensated procurement characterized only as donation | dual disclosure of compensated commercial procurement and biological-product transfer | exact public language; transaction economics | avoid declaring all use of donation unlawful absent authority |
| regulation implied to guarantee individualized safety | disclose scope and limits of regulatory compliance | representations; operative regulations; expert explanation | do not imply regulation is irrelevant or absent |
| eligibility represented as medical advisability | explicit distinction between collection eligibility and individualized longitudinal medical assessment | screening protocol; consent; medical testimony | define what assessment is and is not provided |
| testing represented as comprehensive monitoring | disclose each test's purpose and provide longitudinal record access | test menu, SOP, data system, participant materials | protect legitimate confidential and product-quality functions |
| deferral represented as adequate remedy | disclose that deferral may stop collection without diagnosis, treatment, compensation, or neutral adjudication | deferral protocol and communications | account for actual referrals or services provided |
| inaccessible longitudinal data | participant-readable procedure, volume, laboratory, deferral, and reaction history | system fields, export capability, privacy analysis | allow secure authenticated delivery and corrections |
| incomplete cumulative-risk disclosure | revised consent and periodic cumulative-participation disclosure | expert review, literature, internal risk documents | avoid court-ordered medical assertions unsupported by consensus |
| internal-only complaint structure | neutral escalation, written outcomes, record correction, preservation, and regulator-contact information | current procedure and outcomes | remedy must be administrable and not create unauthorized tribunal |

| standardized deception across facilities | independent compliance audit and periodic reporting | statewide standardization and recurrence | define duration, scope, auditor, confidentiality, and termination |

## Personal relief

Personal relief must be calculated separately and may include only legally recoverable, proven items such as:

- payments promised but not made;
- transportation, communication, and transaction costs caused by actionable deception;
- lost compensation or other economic loss causally linked to wrongful deferral or misrepresentation;
- medical expenses and bodily injury supported by competent causation evidence;
- consequential loss not speculative or duplicative;
- statutory costs, investigation expenses, attorney fees, and interest where authorized.

No statewide industry revenue, total plasma value, or structural fund amount is Plaintiff's personal damages.

## Restitution and accounting

Before seeking restitution, disgorgement, or accounting, establish:

1. the specific benefit received from Plaintiff or the identified affected transactions;
2. the legal basis for the remedy under each pleaded claim;
3. whether an adequate legal remedy or contract displaces equitable relief;
4. the proper measure—payment differential, unjustly retained benefit, traceable proceeds, avoided cost, or another legally recognized measure;
5. nonduplication with compensatory damages;
6. an administrable claimant-identification and distribution process; and
7. treatment of unclaimed funds consistent with governing law.

## Structural relief pool

The proposed Minnesota Structural Relief Pool of not less than $3.6 billion remains a remedial hypothesis, not an established entitlement or present damages calculation.

It may advance only after proof of:

- jurisdiction and equitable authority;
- duration and scope of the standardized conduct;
- Minnesota participant and transaction counts;
- collected volumes and downstream value;
- incidence and cost of medically indicated assessment or monitoring;
- notice, disclosure, records-access, audit, and administration costs;
- restitution methodology;
- causal and proportional relationship between misconduct and amount;
- avoidance of punitive or duplicative recovery absent authority; and
- a governance structure protecting participants and judicial control.

Potential components, subject to authority and proof:

1. participant notice and records delivery;
2. independent longitudinal medical assessment for defined eligible participants;
3. laboratory and extraction-history reconstruction;
4. claims administration and restitution;
5. independent compliance and data audit;
6. corrective advertising and consent redesign;
7. neutral complaint and record-correction administration;
8. research or surveillance limited to lawful, consented, privacy-protected purposes; and
9. court reporting and sunset review.

## Injunction drafting controls

Every injunction term must state:

- the defendant bound;
- the exact prohibited or required conduct;
- the covered communication, facility, participant group, and geography;
- the implementation date;
- objective compliance criteria;
- record-retention and audit requirements;
- reporting frequency and recipient;
- treatment of confidential information;
- dispute-resolution and modification process;
- duration and termination standard; and
- legal basis.

Avoid commands to "comply with the law" without operational specificity.

## Public-correction evidence plan

| Proof category | Examples |
|---|---|
| public reach | advertisements, websites, app screens, referral and bonus campaigns, signs |
| standardization | common SOPs, form versions, scripts, centralized systems, training |
| continuation | current materials, active facilities, recurring promotions, unchanged protocols |
| materiality | participant testimony, design research, marketing analysis, expert evidence |
| falsity or omission | actual safeguard purpose, internal limits, data architecture, regulatory text |
| feasible correction | existing data exports, alternative disclosures, audit mechanisms |
| public effect | number of facilities, participants, transactions, prospective exposure |

## Proposed declaration subjects

- Plaintiff participation and exposure to specific representations;
- participant-facing materials and what was not disclosed;
- transportation, time, compensation, and deferral consequences;
- machine-display observations stated without unsupported precision;
- standardized public advertising;
- expert explanation of procedure, tests, cumulative risk, and limitations;
- data expert explanation of longitudinal records and feasible participant access;
- economist explanation of compensation design, transaction value, restitution, and remedial cost;
- compliance expert explanation of safeguards, audit, and corrective architecture.
