# Preservation and Discovery Package

Date: 2026-08-05
Matter: MAT-PLASMA-2026-08-04-001

## A. Preservation demand scope

Preserve all paper and electronically stored information from the earliest relevant participation date through final resolution, including metadata, audit trails, deleted data, archived versions, backups, system logs, and third-party-controlled records within Defendants' possession, custody, or control.

### Custodians and systems

- Plaintiff's complete donor/participant file
- facility manager, quality manager, medical director, responsible physician, phlebotomy and floor staff
- regional and national operations, medical, quality, regulatory, marketing, compensation, finance, legal, compliance, data, and device teams
- donor-management systems, scheduling systems, mobile applications, payment systems, laboratory interfaces, complaint systems, CAPA/deviation systems, learning systems, email, chat, document repositories, and device databases

### Subject matter

1. enrollment, identity, consent, eligibility, screening, physical examinations, vital signs, questionnaires, and education;
2. every attempted and completed procedure, date, time, staff member, station, needle, machine, kit, lot, target, actual collected plasma, processed blood, anticoagulant, saline, alarms, pauses, overrides, terminations, and reactions;
3. weight measurements, scales, calibration, nomogram inputs, category changes, volume calculations, device software, configuration, validation, and updates;
4. all laboratory samples, raw results, corrected results, interfaces, review, notifications, thresholds, trend analysis, protein, albumin, immunoglobulin, hematology, infectious-disease, and deferral records;
5. all deferrals, reason codes, duration, re-entry decisions, appeals, exceptions, physician review, and communications;
6. compensation schedules, bonuses, lotteries, referral programs, promotional rules, behavioral analyses, payment records, and changes;
7. advertising, websites, applications, signs, scripts, FAQs, consent forms, educational materials, and every historical version;
8. complaints, adverse reactions, incidents, investigations, deviations, CAPA, regulatory reports, insurance notices, claims, and litigation holds;
9. staffing, credentials, training, competency, supervision, performance metrics, throughput expectations, discipline, and incentives;
10. internal studies, risk reviews, literature reviews, cumulative-effect analyses, participant-retention analysis, repeat-participation data, and longitudinal monitoring capabilities;
11. FDA inspections, communications, Form 483 observations, warning letters, biologics-license materials, recalls, deviations, and responses;
12. contracts, transfers, invoices, pricing, fractionation, downstream disposition, and valuation of collected plasma; and
13. corporate ownership, control, agency, licensing, insurance, indemnity, and responsibility allocation.

## C. Interrogatories

1. Identify every legal entity and trade name involved in the challenged Minnesota operation and describe each role.
2. Identify every facility where Plaintiff participated and the operator, license holder, medical director, and responsible physician for each period.
3. State every visit, procedure, target amount, actual amount, processed volume, anticoagulant amount, machine, software version, alarm, and termination associated with Plaintiff.
4. Describe every test performed on Plaintiff, its purpose, threshold, reviewing person, notification process, and resulting decision.
5. State every deferral imposed on Plaintiff, its factual basis, code, duration, review, re-entry criteria, and communication.
6. Describe how Defendants distinguish product-suitability testing, collection eligibility, immediate safety screening, and longitudinal participant-health monitoring.
7. Describe every system used to detect cumulative risk or adverse trends across repeated procedures.
8. Identify every standardized representation concerning donation, safety, regulation, medical supervision, testing, eligibility, deferral, remedies, complaint procedures, and monitoring.
9. Describe all compensation and repeat-participation incentive programs applicable to Plaintiff and Minnesota participants.
10. State Minnesota aggregate participant, procedure, collected-volume, deferral, adverse-event, and complaint data for the relevant period.
11. Identify every internal study or review of cumulative effects, participant vulnerability, economic inducement, or disclosure sufficiency.
12. Describe all bases for denying that the challenged practices are commercial transactions or that the representations serve a public-facing inducement function.
13. Identify all facts supporting each affirmative defense.
14. Identify all persons with knowledge and all documents supporting each response.

## D. Requests for admission

1. Admit Defendants compensate participants for completed plasma-collection procedures.
2. Admit Defendants obtain title to or commercial control over collected plasma.
3. Admit Defendants use standardized consent and eligibility materials.
4. Admit Defendants use public advertising or promotions to induce participation.
5. Admit a participant may satisfy collection eligibility criteria without receiving a comprehensive longitudinal medical assessment.
6. Admit temporary deferral does not itself diagnose the cause of an abnormal laboratory value.
7. Admit temporary deferral does not itself provide treatment or compensation for a participant's abnormal value.
8. Admit some testing is performed for product suitability or regulatory release purposes.
9. Admit Defendants retain longitudinal procedure and laboratory data not automatically delivered to participants as an integrated analysis.
10. Admit internal complaint or adverse-event reporting does not automatically create a neutral adjudication or damages remedy for the participant.
11. Admit Defendants determine compensation schedules and repeat-participation incentives.
12. Admit machine-generated records identify target or actual collection information for individual sessions.
13. Admit Defendants can identify the machine, software, nomogram, and staff associated with Plaintiff's sessions.
14. Admit Defendants possess historical versions of relevant public and participant-facing materials, or state specifically which versions were not retained.

## G. Discovery integrity controls

- Request native data plus human-readable exports.
- Require field definitions, code dictionaries, time zones, units, and audit histories.
- Preserve original metadata and chain of custody.
- Separate Plaintiff-specific production from deidentified aggregate production.
- Negotiate a protective order without surrendering use of evidence needed for public injunctive claims.
- Challenge conclusory privilege logs and require document-by-document information.
- Track each production item to complaint paragraph, element, defense, and remedy.
