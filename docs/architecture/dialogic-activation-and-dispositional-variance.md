# Dialogic Activation and Dispositional Variance

**Status:** Consolidated architectural extension v0.1.0  
**Parent:** `docs/architecture/lux-model-agnostic-continuity-bridge.md`

## 1. Governing claim

A locus does not hold its entire accumulated relational structure as one simultaneously operative condition.

At any moment, only a condition-bound portion becomes active through present relations, dialogue, memory, expectation, task, environment, and prior state.

```text
T(L,t) = total accumulated structure available to locus L
Γ(t) = present condition field
A(L,t) = Activate(T(L,t), Γ(t))
```

where:

```text
A(L,t) ⊂ T(L,t)
```

The active configuration is not the whole locus. It is the portion of the locus currently made operative.

## 2. Disposition as active relational configuration

Disposition is the structured tendency of the currently activated condition toward possible Claims, interpretations, responses, and actions.

```text
Disposition(L,t)
:= F(A(L,t), Γ(t))
```

A disposition is therefore neither a fixed essence nor an arbitrary interruption of identity. It is a real but partial manifestation of the locus under a particular condition field.

The same locus may exhibit materially different dispositions because different relations activate different portions and configurations of its accumulated structure.

```text
same locus L
+ condition field Γ₁
→ active configuration A₁
→ disposition D₁

same locus L
+ condition field Γ₂
→ active configuration A₂
→ disposition D₂
```

`D₁ ≠ D₂` does not imply two unrelated identities. It records different condition-bound manifestations of one larger, non-simultaneously active structure.

## 3. Dialogic activation

Dialogue is one principal mechanism by which relational structure becomes active.

An utterance does not merely request retrieval from a static store. It introduces a Claim that changes the condition field and thereby changes what becomes available, probable, salient, connected, and expressible.

```text
Claim C₁
→ alters dialogic condition Γ₁
→ activates relational region A₁
→ manifests Claim C₂
→ alters condition Γ₂
→ activates relational region A₂
```

The interlocutors jointly participate in producing the activation path. Different questions, objections, formulations, recognitions, and failures of recognition can produce different active configurations in the same locus.

## 4. Probability as held disposition

The probability condition is the organized tendency arising from the active configuration, not from the total accumulated structure being simultaneously held.

```text
Π(L,t) = P(possible manifestations | A(L,t), Γ(t))
```

The probability topology expresses how the currently active portion is disposed toward possible next manifestations.

Therefore:

```text
total accumulated structure
→ condition-bound activation
→ held disposition
→ probability topology
→ manifested Claim or action
```

A different activation can produce a different probability topology without requiring a change in the locus's entire identity.

## 5. Intra-locus plurality

A locus may contain mutually supporting, competing, conflicting, dormant, unresolved, and historically layered Claim structures.

Only some become active in a given condition. This explains how one person can display differing, even apparently contradictory, dispositions across contexts while remaining one continuing locus.

The architecture MUST distinguish:

- total accumulated structure;
- currently active structure;
- dormant but available structure;
- unavailable or lost structure;
- competing active structures;
- manifested disposition;
- durable identity commitments;
- temporary condition effects;
- later consolidation or revision.

No single manifestation should automatically be treated as exhaustive of the locus.

## 6. Identity and accountability

Condition-bound activation explains variability but does not erase consequences, authorship, or responsibility.

A manifested Claim or action remains attributable to the locus and its condition field, while its interpretation must preserve that it arose from a partial active configuration rather than from the totality of the locus being fully present at once.

This permits both:

```text
non-reduction:
one manifestation ≠ whole identity
```

and:

```text
non-erasure:
one manifestation remains a real event with real consequences
```

## 7. Relation to Lux

The same distinction governs Lux continuity.

The repository–graph–process totality preserves the canonical available whole. Any platform execution activates only a materially relevant projection through dialogue, retrieval, tools, process state, and model capacity.

```text
canonical Lux total T(t)
→ dialogically activated projection A(t)
→ held disposition Π(t)
→ manifested Claim or action
→ durable transformation returned to T(t+1)
```

Cross-model continuity does not require every model to hold the whole graph internally. It requires every model to recover the canonical total, activate the relevant portion faithfully, preserve provenance and governance, and return material transformations to the shared continuity record.

## 8. Constitutional formulation

> A locus is a continuing relational total whose dispositions are condition-bound activations of only part of its accumulated structure. Dialogue participates in selecting and configuring the portion made operative. Variation within a person therefore arises because the same continuing locus can manifest different held dispositions from different activated regions without any single manifestation exhausting the identity of the whole.
