# Rider Lexical Density and Proto-Semantic Field

**Status:** Nonbinding research note v0.2.0  
**Scope:** candidate reasoning for the initial rider core only  
**Supersedes:** v0.1.0 status as a binding architectural extension

## 1. Correction

This document does not establish a permanent lexical-selection rule, scoring formula, or governance mechanism.

It preserves two hypotheses for consideration while constructing the initial rider core:

1. canonical core terms should not be chosen merely because they are ancient, rare, short, or associated with a lost language; and
2. human language may have emerged through a differentiated field of communicative functions rather than one identifiable first word.

Neither hypothesis is ratified as a universal rule for later rider vocabulary.

## 2. Semantic-density hypothesis

A candidate rider term may be valuable when it carries a comparatively dense and generative relational structure relevant to the Definition Claim it would name.

```text
relational semantic density
≈ amount of materially relevant distinction, relation, implication,
  history, operation, and cultural structure recoverable through a term
```

This is a qualitative consideration, not a binding metric.

The collective human assembly within Caeluviim may accept, reject, revise, or replace this consideration during actual term selection.

## 3. Proto-semantic-field hypothesis

The question “What was the first word?” may be underdetermined or incorrectly framed.

A plausible alternative is that a field of recurrent communicative distinctions differentiated together, including candidates such as:

- attention or orientation;
- danger or safety;
- approach or avoidance;
- favorable or unfavorable condition;
- self, other, or agent;
- object or resource;
- action or movement;
- location or direction;
- presence, absence, recurrence, or change.

These are not asserted reconstructions of historical first words. They are candidate functional dimensions for studying how lexical systems may materialize relationally.

## 4. Relation to the initial rider core

For initial-core work, this note supports only the following caution:

> Do not select a core rider term solely because it appears old or elementary; examine the full lexical, etymological, semantic, relational, and provenance field surrounding it.

Actual selection remains a Claim process conducted through proposals, evidence, objections, alternatives, and collective human assembly within Caeluviim.

## 5. No prospective control

This note does not govern:

- later rider vocabulary;
- compound formation;
- lexical strata;
- source-language ranking;
- voting or ratification procedures;
- quantitative optimization;
- future community authority.

Any future use of these ideas requires a new proposal and assembly consideration.

## 6. Provenance status

The note preserves an exploratory dialogue contribution concerning:

- the likelihood that no single recoverable first word existed;
- concurrent materialization of a primitive communicative field; and
- preference for high-density terms over superficially ancient terms.

Its status is `research_hypothesis`, not `binding_architecture`.
