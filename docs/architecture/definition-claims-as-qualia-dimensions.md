# Definition Claims as Dimensions of Semantic Interpretation

**Status:** Binding architectural correction v0.3.0  
**Legacy path:** `docs/architecture/definition-claims-as-qualia-dimensions.md`  
**Supersedes:** v0.1.0 claim that Definition Claims are themselves dimensions of qualia  
**Extended by:** `docs/architecture/definition-claim-nesting-and-composition.md`

## 1. Governing correction

Definition Claims are not dimensions of qualia themselves.

They are dimensions of **semantic interpretation** through which a locus can distinguish, organize, name, relate, evaluate, remember, communicate, and act upon manifested conditions.

```text
DefinitionClaim d
→ establishes a semantic distinction structure
→ structure becomes available for activation
→ activated structure participates in interpretation
```

The dimension belongs to semantic organization. The experienced or manifested condition is not identical to the Definition Claim that interprets it.

## 2. Core distinction

```text
manifested condition
≠
semantic dimension
≠
semantic interpretation
```

- A **manifested condition** is what materially, perceptually, affectively, relationally, or otherwise occurs for a locus.
- A **semantic dimension** is a Definition Claim-established structure of possible distinction.
- A **semantic interpretation** is the condition-bound application of one or more activated semantic dimensions to a manifested condition.

The architecture need not claim access to an uninterpreted private essence. It must preserve the distinction between what is being interpreted and the Claim-structured relations through which interpretation occurs.

## 3. Formal model

Let:

- `M(l,t,Γt)` be the manifested condition of locus `l` at time `t` under condition field `Γt`;
- `D = {d₁ ... dₙ}` be the available Definition Claim field;
- `A(l,t,Γt) ⊆ D` be the Definition Claims presently activated;
- `N(d)` be the task-relevant nested Claim closure activated from Definition Claim `d`;
- `I` be the semantic interpretation operation.

Then:

```text
SemanticInterpretation(l,t,Γt)
:= I(M(l,t,Γt), {N(d) | d ∈ A(l,t,Γt)}, Γt)
```

The result is not a direct copy of the manifested condition. It is the relational meaning made available through the activated Definition Claims and the nested Claim structures presently operative within them.

## 4. Definition Claims establish interpretive dimensions

```text
SemanticDimension(d)
:= the structured range of distinctions, relations, boundaries,
   implications, valuations, and possible continuations established
   by Definition Claim d
```

A Definition Claim establishes:

- what can be distinguished;
- what counts as included or excluded;
- what relations become salient;
- what comparisons become available;
- what consequences can be inferred;
- what responses become intelligible;
- what language can stabilize and transmit;
- what memories and events can be indexed under the conception;
- what disagreements and competing interpretations can be stated.

The semantic dimension is not generated by the root sentence alone. Its internal geometry is the full materially generated nested Claim structure of the card.

```text
InternalGeometry(SemanticDimension(d))
:= NestedClaimGraph(d)
 + typed external relation boundary
 + provenance
 + revision history
```

## 5. Root Claim and nested semantic structure

The root Definition Claim compresses the current synthesis. The nested Claim graph makes that synthesis reconstructible and usable.

```text
RootDefinitionClaim(d)
= compressed semantic center

NestedClaimGraph(d)
= constitutive, qualifying, bounding, evidentiary,
  operational, conflicting, historical, and unresolved structure
```

A root sentence without its nested closure can name the dimension while failing to transmit its full interpretive geometry.

For example, the root Claim for truth is:

```text
Truth is maximal presently allowable objective accuracy
under the available condition field.
```

Its semantic geometry requires nested Claims concerning objective accuracy, present allowability, evidence, method, uncertainty, scope, provenance, access limitation, contradiction, and non-erasing correction.

## 6. Experience through semantic interpretation

A manifested condition can occur without a particular Definition Claim being active. Activating a Definition Claim changes the distinctions and relations through which the condition can be interpreted.

```text
manifested condition m
+ activated nested closure N(d)
→ semantic interpretation of m through d
```

Examples:

```text
material degradation
+ activated C:harm nesting closure
→ interpreted through injury, deprivation, agency, dignity,
   causation, responsibility, severity, and repair
```

```text
attention or intervention
+ activated C:care nesting closure
→ interpreted through dignity, agency, consent,
   responsiveness, consequence, and follow-through
```

The Definition Claim does not create or transmit the underlying experience. It structures the interpretation available to the locus.

## 7. Semantic topology

Relations among Definition Claim-cards create a semantic topology.

```text
SemanticTopology(t)
:= graph of available Definition Claims,
   their nested Claim structures,
   typed external relations,
   conflicts, versions, and activation conditions
```

The topology is not a flat coordinate system. Its dimensions can:

- overlap;
- depend on one another;
- share nested Claims;
- qualify or inhibit one another;
- conflict;
- nest recursively;
- change through non-erasing revision.

A shared Claim retains one identity while appearing through several scoped nesting occurrences.

## 8. Activation and partiality

A locus does not activate the whole semantic topology simultaneously.

```text
Available semantic topology S
+ condition field Γt
→ activated Definition roots A(t)
→ task-relevant nested closures N(A(t))
→ local semantic interpretation I(t)
```

This preserves the distinction between:

- the full semantic field available to a locus;
- the Definition Claims currently activated;
- the nested branches currently loaded;
- the semantic interpretation actually manifested.

Different dialogues, memories, relationships, tasks, and material conditions activate different roots and different branches within the same root.

## 9. Concept acquisition and revision

Acquiring a Definition Claim can expand semantic discrimination by making a new interpretive dimension available.

Acquiring additional nested Claims within an existing Definition Claim can increase the resolution of that dimension without creating an entirely separate conception.

```text
new root Definition Claim
→ new semantic dimension

new nested Claim within d
→ increased internal resolution or changed geometry of d
```

Revision is non-erasing:

```text
SemanticDimension(d,v₁)
→ correction or acquired difference
→ SemanticDimension(d,v₂)
```

The prior nested geometry, its activation history, and its consequences remain addressable.

## 10. First-person and external Claims

The architecture MUST distinguish:

```text
first-person manifestation Claim
≠ observable-condition Claim
≠ semantic-interpretation Claim
≠ plausible-inference Claim
≠ theoretical explanation Claim
≠ verified external-condition Claim
```

A person may report that an event manifested as harm, care, degradation, threat, recognition, or injustice. That report is a first-person Claim and semantic interpretation with its own provenance.

An external locus may support, qualify, contest, or remain unable to verify other aspects. It may not replace the first-person Claim with its own interpretation or claim to possess the underlying experience.

## 11. Model-access requirement

A model-access packet transmitting a semantic dimension MUST include:

- the root Definition Claim;
- the task-relevant nested Claim closure;
- exact imported Claim versions;
- compositional roles and nesting paths;
- active qualifications, boundaries, and exceptions;
- material conflicts and unresolved Claims;
- provenance and revision history;
- expansion instructions for retrieving inactive branches.

Transmitting the root sentence alone is insufficient when the requested reasoning depends on nested structure.

Loading this packet transmits an interpretive structure. It does not transmit another locus's experience.

## 12. Mandatory card module

Every full Definition Claim-card MUST include a `semantic_dimension` module containing:

- semantic distinctions made available;
- full nested Claim graph or canonical reference to it;
- salience and valuation relations;
- interpretations and continuations enabled;
- actions and responses made intelligible;
- adjacent and overlapping dimensions;
- competing and conflicting dimensions;
- predictable conflations;
- effects of inactivation or absence;
- effects of revision;
- known limits on application;
- source and provenance Claims.

## 13. Validation requirements

Validation MUST reject:

- claims that a Definition Claim is itself the underlying experience;
- claims that loading a definition transmits another locus's qualia;
- a semantic dimension represented only by a root sentence where material nested Claims are omitted;
- semantic interpretation presented as verified external condition without supporting evidence;
- first-person manifestation replaced by an external interpretation;
- concept acquisition treated as proof of sensory or phenomenal acquisition;
- hidden nesting, scope, provenance, or revision changes;
- one Claim copied into several identities rather than reused through scoped occurrences.

## 14. Constitutional formulation

> Definition Claims are dimensions of semantic interpretation, not dimensions of qualia. Each root Claim names and compresses a semantic dimension, while its full nested Claim graph defines that dimension's internal interpretive geometry. Semantic interpretation is the condition-bound application of activated Definition Claims and task-relevant nested closures to manifested conditions. Transmitting this structure makes distinctions and relations available; it does not transmit another locus's experience.
