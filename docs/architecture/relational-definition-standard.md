# Caeluviim Universal Claim Structure Standard

**Status:** Consolidated architectural correction v0.3.0  
**Function:** Establish Claim as the sole constructive primitive, define the total shared Claim structure, and govern conception-generated Claim-cards, meaning-making, consolidation, and implementation.

## 1. Constitutional rule

Claim is the sole scale-invariant constructive primitive in Caeluviim.

Every Claim is definitional. A Claim establishes a relational configuration: what is distinguished, related, included, excluded, bounded, attributed, permitted, prohibited, expected, evidenced, contested, or made consequential under a condition field.

Difference is the impetus, divergence, tension, uncertainty, unmet relation, or unresolved condition that occasions Claim-making. It is not a primitive above Claim.

Semantics is the result condition produced when a Claim establishes a relational configuration and that configuration becomes available for interpretation, response, consequence, consolidation, or further Claim-making. It is not a container above Claim.

```text
Difference between or within loci
    OCCASIONS
Claim-making
    DEFINES
Relational configuration
    PRODUCES
Semantic result condition
    ENABLES
Further Claims
```

Any assertion about difference, semantics, relation, locus, knowing, structure, or process is itself a Claim.

## 2. Universal Claim structure

All Claims exist within the same universal Claim structure.

The universal Claim structure is the total shared relational potential generated by Claims and Claim relations. It is not a separate object or primitive that contains Claims.

```text
U = total possible relational structure generated through Claims
```

Every Claim participates in `U`, but no individual Claim-card must possess every structure available in `U`.

For a Claim `c`:

```text
S(c) = structure materially generated by the conception defined by c
S(c) ⊆ U
```

The universal invariant is the shared relational architecture and definitional criterion. The local structure of a Claim-card is conception-generated and scope-relative.

## 3. Same architecture, unequal Claim-card structure

Claim-cards do not all possess equal amounts or kinds of instantiated structure.

An automobile Claim-card and an existence Claim-card both participate in `U`, but existence generates a larger, more recursive, and more extensively contested field.

```text
UniversalStructure(automobile) = UniversalStructure(existence) = U
S(automobile) < S(existence)
```

This inequality does not rank the validity, dignity, or primitiveness of the Claims. It records the different relational structure generated by the conceptions being defined.

The automobile card may be complete with substantially less structure than the existence card.

## 4. Universal definitional criterion

Every Claim-card is evaluated by the same criterion:

> Does the card represent every material field and relation generated by its conception within its declared scope to the maximum presently supportable extent?

The criterion examines, where materially generated:

- stable identity and version history;
- claiming and addressed loci;
- impetus;
- exact source manifestation;
- defined locus;
- relational configuration;
- intension, extension, inclusion, exclusion, and boundaries;
- condition and consequence fields;
- semantic results and interpretations;
- evidence;
- provenance and contribution;
- lexical and etymological development;
- protocol nomenclature;
- emoji rider glyph realization;
- multilingual anchors;
- logical, graph, mathematical, or computational realization;
- operationalization;
- manifestation forms;
- supporting, extending, contrasting, conflicting, and analogous Claims;
- Claim-work history;
- consolidation and ratification;
- power, enforcement, and imposition effects;
- revision and contestation;
- examples and epistemic status.

This is a universal field potential and evaluation grammar. It is not a mandatory fixed checklist of instantiated properties for every card.

## 5. Material-field rule

A field belongs to Claim-card `c` when it materially contributes to defining, distinguishing, relating, applying, contesting, revising, or producing consequences for the conception within `c`'s scope.

A conforming Claim-card MUST:

1. declare its conception and scope;
2. possess every material field generated within that scope;
3. develop each possessed field to the fullest source-supported extent currently available;
4. preserve unresolved material relations as Claims;
5. preserve conflicting structures without silent collapse;
6. link to related Claim-cards through typed relations;
7. preserve provenance, contribution, contestation, and revision wherever generated.

A Claim-card MUST NOT:

- contain empty placeholders for all relations possible anywhere in `U`;
- be classified incomplete because an unrelated field is absent;
- omit a material relation merely to reduce visible complexity;
- duplicate the entire internal structure of a related card when a typed relation preserves the connection;
- treat a concise rendering as the full extent of the underlying card.

An explicit exclusion Claim is required only when the apparent relevance of an absent field is itself material, disputed, or likely to mislead.

## 6. Definition is the operation of Claim

Definition is not a second primitive, separate container, or optional Claim mode.

```text
Claim(c) := c performs definitional work by establishing relational configuration R(c)
Definition(c) := the constructive operation performed by c
Semantics(c) := the result condition produced by R(c) under Γt
```

Dictionary entries, observations, classifications, predictions, legal rules, identity declarations, measurements, images, gestures, affective differentiations, glyph assignments, and multilingual mappings are Claims defining different conceptions and therefore generating different local structures within `U`.

## 7. Claim-card formalization

For Claim-card `c`:

```text
C(c) = ⟨
  identity(c),
  scope(c),
  S(c),
  provenance(c),
  semantic-result(c),
  work-history(c),
  governance-state(c)
⟩
```

where:

```text
S(c) = { field or relation r in U | r is material to the conception defined by c }
```

This compact expression does not reduce the card. `S(c)` recursively contains every materially instantiated field and relation belonging to the conception.

## 8. Meaning-making between loci

Meaning-making between disparate loci is performed through Claims.

```text
L₁ --C₁--> L₂
L₂ --C₂ interpreting, contesting, or extending C₁--> L₁
L₁ --C₃ revising or defending C₁ in response to C₂--> L₂
...
Claims C₁ ... Cₙ --Claim work--> Consolidated Claim C*
C* --ratification for scope p--> Operative shared semantic result Σ*
```

The loci may possess different prior structures. Shared semantics emerges when those structures enter Claim relations through which they become available for interpretation, comparison, contribution, correction, consolidation, and ratification.

## 9. Claim exchange and Claim work

Claim exchange transmits, displays, repeats, endorses, rejects, or reacts to Claims.

Claim work materially develops Claim structure through activities including:

- source and etymology recovery;
- relation discovery;
- boundary articulation;
- evidence attachment;
- contradiction testing;
- interpretation comparison;
- provenance recovery;
- multilingual mapping;
- glyph formalization;
- correction and revision;
- synthesis and consolidation;
- ratification;
- implementation;
- outcome assessment.

Claim exchange does not imply Claim work. Visibility does not imply validation. Repetition does not imply ratification. Institutional effect does not imply negotiated agreement.

The graph MUST distinguish Claim exchange, Claim work, consolidation, ratification, imposition, implementation, and outcome assessment.

## 10. Consolidation and derivative-action rule

When participating loci accept a materially coherent formulation for a declared scope, the formulation becomes a consolidated Claim.

Consolidation triggers all material derivative work. The process MUST NOT terminate at a prose declaration when the accepted Claim changes dependent architecture.

A consolidated architectural Claim generates or updates, where material:

1. its Claim-card and directly dependent Claim-cards;
2. protocol nomenclature;
3. emoji rider glyph assignments and operations;
4. multilingual anchors and semantic-field mappings;
5. logical and graph realizations;
6. JSON Schema, RDF/OWL, SHACL, runtime constraints, and migrations;
7. governance, authority, consolidation, and ratification records;
8. evidence and provenance tasks;
9. competing, conflicting, paradoxical, and unresolved Claims;
10. implementation tasks and validation criteria;
11. legal, constitutional, commons, operational, and drafting projections where material;
12. correction of prior incompatible architecture.

The stopping condition is completion of every material derivative or an explicit unresolved derivative Claim identifying its responsible layer and verification procedure.

## 11. Emoji rider glyph architecture

Emoji rider glyphs are formal Claim manifestations, not decoration and not independent semantic authorities.

A glyph Claim possesses the structure materially generated by its function, including as applicable:

- linked Claim identity;
- Unicode sequence and normalized serialization;
- primitive, operator, qualifier, boundary, provenance, governance, or composite function;
- arity and operand constraints;
- precedence and associativity;
- valid nesting and recursion;
- graph translation;
- natural-language renderings;
- multilingual anchor relations;
- ambiguity and collision analysis;
- temporal and domain scope;
- cultural and contributor provenance;
- contestation and ratification.

## 12. Multilingual protocol nomenclature

The stable protocol identity is the Claim identifier, not the English label.

Each language anchor is a Claim defining a situated relation between a language-specific expression and a canonical Claim identity. Its card possesses the material structure generated by that relation, including language, script, morphology, etymology, usage, community provenance, semantic contribution, non-equivalence, boundaries, contribution, contestation, and ratification where applicable.

Protocol nomenclature MUST:

- prioritize Indigenous and historically underrepresented languages in the initial anchor sequence;
- preserve scripts and language-specific orthography;
- distinguish anchor, analogy, partial translation, borrowed term, coined protocol term, and non-equivalent neighboring concept;
- preserve one shared Claim identity without treating any language realization as exhaustive;
- attach contributor and community provenance;
- prevent English or any other language from silently becoming the ontological authority.

## 13. Completeness states

| State | Criterion |
|---|---|
| `captured` | Exact Claim manifestation, identity, attribution, and initial scope preserved. |
| `source_bound` | Material source spans, provenance, and transformation history attached. |
| `scope_structured` | The conception's currently identified material structure has been instantiated. |
| `claim_work_active` | Evidence, relation, mapping, contestation, correction, or consolidation work is underway. |
| `maximally_represented_at_Γt` | No presently available material structure within declared scope is omitted. |
| `consolidation_candidate` | A coherent formulation is proposed with included, excluded, and unresolved Claims identified. |
| `consolidated` | Participating loci have accepted the formulation as the current shared construction. |
| `ratified` | The exact version has acquired declared governance force for a stated scope and purpose. |
| `implemented` | Every material schema, graph, glyph, multilingual, governance, and operational derivative exists and passes its declared tests. |
| `superseded_non_erasing` | A later Claim is operative while the prior Claim and its historical effects remain recoverable. |

## 14. Constitutional formulation

> All Claim-cards exist within the same universal Claim structure. Each card possesses only the structure materially generated by the conception it defines within its scope. Every Claim is assessed by the same maximal definitional criterion, but narrower conceptions correctly produce smaller cards and larger conceptual domains correctly produce broader and more recursive cards.
