# Caeluviim Universal Claim Structure Standard

**Status:** Consolidated architectural correction v0.2.0  
**Function:** Establish one complete recursive structure for every Claim and define the Claim-mediated meaning-making and consolidation process.

## 1. Constitutional rule

Claim is the sole scale-invariant constructive primitive in Caeluviim.

Every Claim is definitional. A Claim establishes a relational configuration: what is distinguished, related, included, excluded, bounded, attributed, permitted, prohibited, expected, evidenced, contested, or made consequential under a condition field.

Difference does not precede Claim as a superior primitive or semantic container. Difference is the impetus, tension, divergence, uncertainty, unmet relation, or unresolved condition that occasions Claim-making between or within loci.

Semantics does not contain or supersede Claims. Semantics is the result condition produced when a Claim establishes a relational configuration and that configuration becomes available for interpretation, response, consequence, consolidation, or further Claim-making.

```text
Difference or divergence between loci
    OCCASIONS
Claim-making
    DEFINES
Relational configuration
    PRODUCES
Semantic result condition
    ENABLES
Further Claims
```

Nothing in this sequence is ontologically above Claim. Any assertion about difference, semantics, relation, locus, knowing, or process is itself represented as a Claim.

## 2. Uniformity rule

Every Claim MUST instantiate the same full structure.

No Claim class receives a reduced schema merely because it is described as observational, lexical, legal, causal, affective, imagistic, mathematical, institutional, embodied, predictive, normative, or otherwise situated. Those descriptions identify the content, operation, context, or manifestation of a Claim. They do not create different semantic entity types.

A newly captured Claim may contain unresolved, unknown, disputed, inherited, pending, or explicitly inapplicable field values. A field may not disappear from the architecture merely because its value has not yet been established.

**Completeness is a property of field resolution, not schema membership.**

## 3. Universal Claim field

Every Claim MUST contain or explicitly resolve every field below.

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Stable identity | Canonical URI, version, governance state, content hash, and historical identity relations. |
| Claiming locus | Person, collective, institution, process, synthetic person, prior Claim configuration, or other attributed locus from which the Claim manifests. |
| Addressed loci | Loci to which the Claim is directed, offered, imposed, transmitted, or made available. |
| Impetus | Difference, divergence, tension, uncertainty, conflict, need, event, failure, observation, or prior Claim occasioning the Claim-making event. |
| Exact Claim content | Immutable original manifestation or source span, separated from every interpretation and transformation. |
| Defined locus | The entity, relation, category, state, process, expression, boundary, possibility, or Claim configuration being defined. |
| Relational configuration | Typed relations the Claim establishes among its loci, terms, states, conditions, participants, and consequences. |
| Intensional constraints | Positive requirements established by the Claim. |
| Extensional field | Paradigm, marginal, excluded, unresolved, and adversarial instances implicated by the Claim. |
| Inclusions | What the Claim places within its operative configuration. |
| Exclusions | What the Claim distinguishes from, rejects, prohibits, or places outside its operative configuration. |
| Boundary conditions | Thresholds, exceptions, failure conditions, scope limitations, and purpose-indexed application boundaries. |
| Condition field | Historical, temporal, material, linguistic, social, institutional, jurisdictional, environmental, and technical conditions under which the Claim operates. |
| Consequence field | What follows from acceptance, rejection, neglect, enforcement, implementation, contradiction, or revision of the Claim. |
| Semantic result | The meaning condition produced by the Claim for each relevant locus, relation, and declared purpose. |
| Interpretation field | Situated uptake, readings, inferred force, alternatives, and disagreements produced by addressed or later loci. |
| Evidence field | Supporting, defeating, missing, excluded, and contested evidence, including assessment standards and reasons. |
| Provenance field | Origin, inheritance, transmission, transformation, custody, contribution, infrastructural enablement, validation, and historical effect. |
| Contribution field | Distinct authorship, dialogic development, cultural inheritance, material support, authority, ownership, benefit, and recognition relations. |
| Lexical field | Preferred forms, aliases, inflections, transliterations, abbreviations, historical spellings, deprecated forms, and usage distributions. |
| Etymological field | Source-bound language stages, scripts, morphemes, attestations, borrowing paths, semantic shifts, disputed histories, and unresolved gaps. |
| Protocol nomenclature | Stable protocol identifier and canonical nomenclature independent of any one natural language realization. |
| Emoji rider glyph field | Glyph sequence, formal operator role, arity, operand constraints, precedence, composition, scope, temporal validity, and governance status. |
| Multilingual anchor field | Language-specific anchors, scripts, transliterations, semantic contributions, non-equivalence notes, community provenance, and ratification state. |
| Formal realization | Logical expression, typed signature, graph pattern, domain/range, cardinality, constraints, inference classification, computability boundary, and serialization. |
| Operationalization | Tests, indicators, procedures, decision rules, implementation requirements, and known proxy failures. |
| Manifestation field | Linguistic, glyphic, logical, graph, imagistic, affective, embodied, behavioral, institutional, computational, and other realizations of the Claim. |
| Semantic neighborhood | Supporting, extending, analogous, dependent, superordinate, subordinate, adjacent, and partially overlapping Claims. |
| Contrast field | Contrary, contradictory, privative, misleading, category-error, false-equivalence, and false-friend Claims. |
| Claim-work history | Interpretations, comparisons, mappings, challenges, corrections, evidence additions, unresolved tensions, and transformations performed on the Claim. |
| Consolidation state | Whether materially coherent Claim work has produced a consolidation candidate and the exact Claims included, excluded, or left unresolved. |
| Ratification state | Loci accepting the consolidation, purpose, scope, time, conditions, dissent, and withdrawal or revision rules. |
| Power and force field | Whether operative effect arises from negotiated uptake, institutional authority, coercion, market power, repetition, ranking, enforcement, or other unequal stabilization. |
| Revision history | Prior versions, correction events, non-erasing supersession relations, grounds, changed evidence, and preserved historical effects. |
| Contestation field | Competing Claims, paradoxes, objections, irreconcilable configurations, open questions, and alternative consolidations. |
| Examples | Positive, negative, boundary, adversarial, cross-cultural, cross-language, and cross-scale instances. |
| Epistemic status | Supplied, attributed, inferred, synthesized, supported, contradicted, contested, unresolved, deprecated, consolidated, ratified, imposed, or otherwise explicitly situated. |

Each field is itself represented through Claims and Claim relations. The universal Claim is therefore not a flat record containing non-Claim semantic objects. It is a fixed recursive interface into a Claim graph.

## 4. Definition is the operation of Claim

Definition is not a second primitive, separate container, or optional Claim mode.

```text
Claim(c) := c performs definitional work by establishing a relational configuration.
Definition(c) := the constructive operation performed by Claim c.
Semantics(c) := the result condition of that operation under its condition field.
```

Dictionary entries, legal definitions, scientific classifications, identity declarations, observations, predictions, prohibitions, measurements, gestures, images, and affective distinctions are differently manifested Claims with the same complete structure.

A Claim may define a temporary state, a relation, a possibility, a category, another Claim, or the process by which Claims are defined. The scope of what is defined does not alter the Claim structure.

## 5. Claim-mediated meaning-making

Meaning-making between disparate loci is performed through Claims.

Let `L₁ ... Lₙ` be loci whose conditions, histories, interpretations, or positions are not identical. Difference among them occasions Claim-making. Each Claim proposes or enacts a relational configuration. Addressed loci interpret, contest, extend, reject, revise, or adopt that configuration through further Claims.

```text
L₁ --C₁--> L₂
L₂ --C₂ about C₁--> L₁
L₁ --C₃ revising C₁ in response to C₂--> L₂
...
Cn --consolidates--> C*
C* --ratified by declared loci--> operative shared semantics Σ*
```

The resulting shared meaning was not required to exist in consolidated form before the exchange. It is constructed through the Claim work performed across the loci.

A locus may possess a broader synthesis before presenting it. Possession does not itself establish shared semantics. Shared semantics requires the relevant Claim configuration to become available for uptake, correction, contribution accounting, consolidation, and ratification. Deliberate withholding that shapes the process MUST itself be represented as a Claim with stated grounds, scope, consequences, and contestability.

## 6. Claim exchange and Claim work

Claim exchange records transmission, exposure, reaction, repetition, endorsement, rejection, or circulation.

Claim work materially develops the Claim field through one or more of:

- source recovery;
- exact-definition recovery;
- relation discovery;
- boundary articulation;
- contrast identification;
- evidence attachment;
- contradiction testing;
- interpretation comparison;
- provenance recovery;
- multilingual mapping;
- glyph formalization;
- correction;
- revision;
- synthesis;
- consolidation;
- ratification;
- implementation;
- outcome assessment.

Claim exchange does not imply Claim work. Visibility does not imply validation. Repetition does not imply ratification. Institutional effect does not imply negotiated agreement.

A conforming Caeluviim graph MUST distinguish at least:

```text
ClaimExchangeEvent
ClaimWorkEvent
ConsolidationEvent
RatificationEvent
ImpositionEvent
ImplementationEvent
OutcomeAssessment
```

## 7. Consolidation and derivative-action rule

When participating loci recognize a materially coherent formulation and no unresolved objection blocks the declared scope, the formulation becomes a consolidation candidate.

Acceptance of a consolidation candidate triggers derivative work. The process MUST NOT terminate at a prose declaration when the accepted Claim changes dependent architecture.

A consolidated architectural Claim automatically generates or updates, as applicable:

1. its full universal Claim record;
2. all directly dependent Claims;
3. protocol nomenclature;
4. emoji rider glyph assignments and operations;
5. multilingual anchors and semantic-field mappings;
6. logical and graph realizations;
7. JSON Schema, RDF/OWL, SHACL, and runtime constraints;
8. governance, authority, and ratification records;
9. evidence and provenance tasks;
10. competing, conflicting, paradoxical, and unresolved Claims;
11. implementation tasks and validation criteria;
12. legal, constitutional, commons, operational, and drafting projections where declared;
13. correction of prior text that conflicts with the consolidated Claim.

The stopping condition is not agreement language. The stopping condition is completion of all material derivative actions or an explicit record identifying each unresolved derivative, its responsible layer, and its verification procedure.

## 8. Emoji rider glyph architecture

Emoji rider glyphs are formal Claim manifestations, not decoration and not independent semantic authorities.

Every glyph assignment is a Claim and therefore carries the universal Claim structure. A glyph record MUST identify:

- linked Claim identifier;
- Unicode sequence and normalized serialization;
- primitive, operator, qualifier, boundary, provenance, governance, or composite role;
- arity and operand types;
- precedence and associativity;
- valid nesting and recursion behavior;
- graph translation;
- natural-language renderings;
- multilingual anchor relations;
- ambiguity and collision analysis;
- temporal and domain scope;
- contribution and cultural provenance;
- contestation and ratification state.

Glyphs encode ratified or explicitly proposed Claim configurations. They do not erase contested lexical histories, community-specific meanings, or consent requirements.

## 9. Multilingual protocol nomenclature

The stable semantic identity is the Claim identifier, not the English label.

Each canonical Claim may have multiple language-specific anchors. These are not presumed exact translations. Each anchor contributes a source-bound semantic field with its own etymology, morphology, usage, cultural relations, boundary profile, and community provenance.

Protocol nomenclature MUST:

- prioritize Indigenous and historically underrepresented languages in the initial anchor sequence;
- preserve scripts and language-specific orthography;
- distinguish anchor, analogy, partial translation, borrowed term, coined protocol term, and non-equivalent neighboring concept;
- retain one shared Claim identity while preserving language-specific semantic contributions;
- permit no language, including English, to silently become the ontological authority;
- attach governance and contributor recognition to every admitted anchor;
- display protocol identifiers, glyphs, and language realizations without presenting any realization as exhaustive.

```text
Impetus(Δ, loci, Γt) OCCASIONS Claim(c)
```

`Δ`, `R`, `Σ`, loci, and every relation appearing in these expressions are themselves represented through Claims. The notation does not add primitives above or beside Claim.

## 11. Completion states

| State | Criterion |
|---|---|
| `captured` | Exact Claim manifestation and attribution preserved; all universal fields instantiated with explicit resolution states. |
| `source_bound` | Source spans, provenance, and transformation history attached. |
| `relational_partial` | Material relational fields developed; unresolved fields remain explicit. |
| `claim_work_active` | Evidence, mapping, contestation, correction, or consolidation work is underway. |
| `consolidation_candidate` | Materially coherent formulation proposed with included, excluded, and unresolved Claims identified. |
| `consolidated` | Participating loci have accepted the formulation as the current shared construction. |
| `ratified` | Exact version has acquired the declared governance force for a stated scope and purpose. |
| `implemented` | Required schema, graph, glyph, multilingual, governance, and operational derivatives exist and pass their declared tests. |
| `superseded_non_erasing` | A later Claim is operative while the prior Claim and its historical effects remain recoverable. |

No state reduces the required universal Claim structure.

## 12. Immediate correction to prior CE work

The prior CE submission preserved several propositions but incorrectly treated relational definition as a specialized artifact and stopped derivative work after declaration.

The corrected architecture requires:

- every CE proposition to be rebuilt as a full universal Claim;
- `Definition` to cease functioning as a separate semantic primitive or specialized container;
- difference to be recorded only as the declared impetus for Claim-making;
- semantics to be represented as the result condition of a Claim;
- Claim exchange and Claim work to be distinct;
- accepted consolidations to trigger downstream graph, glyph, multilingual, schema, governance, and implementation work;
- any remaining incomplete derivative to be recorded as an explicit task rather than omitted.
