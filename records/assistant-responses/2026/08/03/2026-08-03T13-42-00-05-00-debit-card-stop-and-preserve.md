## Substantive response

Insurance does not make the account balance yours. Finding a discarded debit card did not authorize its use.

Stop now. Do not make another purchase, withdrawal, transfer, balance test, digital-wallet addition, or any other attempt with the card or account information.

Minnesota Statutes section 609.821, subdivision 2(1), covers using or attempting to use a financial transaction card without the cardholder's consent while knowing consent was not given. Under subdivision 3(b), transactions or attempts within a six-month period may be aggregated. The possibility that the cardholder may be reimbursed allocates loss between the cardholder and financial institution; it does not authorize the person using the card.

Keep the card secure. Preserve every receipt, transaction record, item, cash amount, message, location, and time connected to the attempt. Do not delete, alter, conceal, transfer, spend, destroy, or discard anything connected to it. Keep any proceeds or goods intact for reversal or restitution.

Contact the issuer using the number printed on the card, or an issuer branch, to report a found card and obtain surrender instructions. Do not make a false statement. Because an attempted use has already occurred, obtain advice from a Minnesota criminal-defense lawyer before giving police, bank investigators, or merchants a detailed factual account.

The assumption that police will not investigate is not a defense and is not a safe operational premise. Banks have federal investigation duties when cardholders report unauthorized electronic transfers, and the reimbursement process does not erase the underlying transaction records.

No assistance will be provided for additional use, extracting funds, transferring proceeds, concealing evidence, or avoiding detection.
