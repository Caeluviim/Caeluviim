# Interpersonal Morality, Care, Harm, Truth, and Mediation Definition Corpus

**Status:** Consolidation candidate v0.1.0  
**Governing standard:** `docs/architecture/standardized-definition-claim-structure.md`  
**Scope:** interpersonal communication, relational ethics, conflict, repair, mediation, and therapeutic-relational engagement  
**Provenance:** dialogic synthesis, user-authored formulations, prior Caeluviim interaction-harm matrices, and repository history

## 1. Governing purpose

This corpus establishes the formal conception field required to reason about interpersonal engagement without collapsing morality, care, harm, consent, conflict, therapy, mediation, responsibility, or repair into one undifferentiated category.

Every entry is a Definition Claim-card. Each definition remains scope-bound, provenance-bearing, contestable, and non-erasing.

The corpus preserves the following user-originated governing formulations:

1. **Truth is maximal presently allowable objective accuracy under the available condition field.**
2. Separate literal content, plausible inference, and verified intent.
3. Attach each apology to a concrete rule change, artifact correction, or validation test.
4. Record the consent basis, scope, and revocability for consequential actions.
5. Limit relational claims to observable acts and current capabilities.
6. Shared history or familiarity does not override present consent or boundaries.
7. Communication necessary for security, coordination, recognition, or care can be withheld through linguistic absence; omission can therefore be materially operative.

## 2. Formal relational expressions

```text
Truth(c, Γt)
:= maximal presently allowable objective accuracy of Claim c
   under available evidence, relations, methods, and condition field Γt
```

```text
Care(a, b, Γt)
:= accurate attention by locus a to locus b
 + preservation of b's dignity and agency
 + consent-sensitive action
 + consequence awareness
 + answerability for effects
```

```text
Harm(x, y, Γt)
:= a materially significant degradation in y's capacity, integrity,
   agency, dignity, standing, relation, opportunity, or condition
   attributable to action, omission, structure, or imposed meaning x
```

```text
Repair(h)
:= truth restoration
 + responsibility attribution
 + affected-artifact and process correction
 + material restoration where possible
 + recurrence prevention
 + preserved provenance of h
```

```text
MoralEvaluation(a, Γt)
:= evaluate consequences, dignity, agency, consent, power,
   reciprocity, responsibility, truth, alternatives, and repairability
   under Γt
```

## 4. Full Definition Claim-cards

### 4.1 Truth

- **Claim ID:** `C:truth`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Truth is maximal presently allowable objective accuracy under the available condition field.
- **Scope:** epistemic assessment, interpersonal communication, institutional Claims, legal Claims, scientific Claims, and model-generated Claims.
- **Relational configuration:** distinguishes truth from confidence, sincerity, consensus, authority, repetition, certainty, and usefulness while relating truth to evidence, method, scope, provenance, and correction.
- **Includes:** presently supportable correspondence; accurate qualification; declared uncertainty; negative findings; preserved contradiction; later correction.
- **Excludes:** final omniscience; truth by authority alone; sincerity as sufficient truth; consensus as sufficient truth; storage as assertion of truth.
- **Boundary conditions:** accuracy is judged against the evidence and methods materially available under `Γt`; missing information limits the allowable claim; later evidence can non-erasingly supersede the current truth Claim.
- **Principal relations:** `SUPPORTED_BY C:evidence`; `CONDITIONED_BY C:condition-field`; `REVISED_BY C:correction`; `DISTINGUISHES_FROM C:certainty`; `GOVERNED_BY C:scope`.
- **Operational rule:** separate literal content, plausible inference, and verified intent.
- **Provenance:** user-authored formulation preserved from prior dialogic synthesis.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidated current definition; literature and formal measurement modules remain extensible.
- **Completeness:** kernel represented; external philosophical comparison and formal correspondence measures pending.

### 4.2 Morality

- **Claim ID:** `C:morality`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Morality is the organized field of Claims concerning what relations, actions, conditions, and consequences ought to be preserved, enabled, constrained, repaired, or refused.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, communal, institutional, technological, ecological, and governance relations.
- **Relational configuration:** relates values, consequences, dignity, agency, consent, power, reciprocity, responsibility, truth, justice, and repair.
- **Includes:** duties; prohibitions; permissions; virtues; consequence assessments; rights; relational obligations; moral uncertainty; competing moral frameworks.
- **Excludes:** morality as mere obedience; morality as whatever an institution enforces; moral certainty without condition or provenance; moral language used to erase material consequences.
- **Boundary conditions:** every moral Claim must identify scope, affected loci, values, anticipated consequences, power relations, alternatives, and revision conditions.
- **Principal relations:** `EVALUATES C:action`; `CONDITIONED_BY C:value`; `CONSTRAINED_BY C:dignity`; `REQUIRES_ACCOUNTING_OF C:harm`; `OPERATIONALIZED_BY C:ethics`.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidation candidate; competing ethical frameworks must remain visible.
- **Completeness:** kernel represented; cross-cultural, historical, religious, philosophical, and empirical modules pending.

### 4.3 Care

- **Claim ID:** `C:care`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Care is sustained, consequence-sensitive attention and action oriented toward preserving or improving another locus's dignity, agency, capacity, and participation according to its actual condition and expressed position.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, caregiving, institutional, technical, therapeutic, and communal relations.
- **Relational configuration:** care joins attention, accurate understanding, consent, action, continuity, consequence, and accountability.
- **Includes:** attentive presence; accurate response; material assistance; protection; coordination; maintenance; repair; respect for refusal; follow-through.
- **Excludes:** self-declared care unsupported by conduct; paternalistic substitution; coercion represented as care; sentiment without consequence awareness; institutional referral as automatic proof of care.
- **Boundary conditions:** the claimed recipient's expressed position and agency remain material; care can require disagreement or refusal but must state its basis and remain accountable for effects.
- **Principal relations:** `PRESERVES C:dignity`; `SUPPORTS C:agency`; `REQUIRES C:listening`; `CONDITIONED_BY C:consent`; `ASSESSED_BY C:consequence`; `REPAIRS C:harm`.
- **Operational rule:** claims of care must be limited to observable acts and current capacities.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidation candidate.
- **Completeness:** kernel represented; care-ethics, caregiving, clinical, disability, and cross-cultural modules pending.

### 4.4 Harm

- **Claim ID:** `C:harm`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Harm is materially significant degradation of capacity, integrity, agency, dignity, standing, relation, opportunity, or condition caused or maintained through action, omission, structure, or imposed meaning.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, physical, semantic, legal, institutional, technological, economic, ecological, and procedural relations.
- **Relational configuration:** connects an affected locus, changed condition, causal or maintaining relation, material threshold, provenance, responsibility, and possible repair.
- **Includes:** direct injury; cumulative degradation; omission; neglect; deprivation; coercive restriction; reputational injury; epistemic injustice; semantic substitution; procedural displacement; institutionalized adverse conditions.
- **Excludes:** mere disagreement; discomfort alone; unverified attribution of intent; any consequence labeled harmful without identifying the degraded capacity or condition.
- **Boundary conditions:** harm does not require malicious intent; causation and responsibility may be distributed; severity, duration, reversibility, foreseeability, dependency, and alternatives are material.
- **Principal relations:** `DEGRADES C:capacity`; `VIOLATES C:dignity`; `CONSTRAINS C:agency`; `CAUSED_BY C:action`; `MAINTAINED_BY C:structure`; `GENERATES_OBLIGATION C:repair`.
- **Operational rule:** preserve literal act, plausible inference, verified intent, experienced consequence, and independently supported consequence as distinct Claims.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidation candidate.
- **Completeness:** kernel represented; domain-specific medical, legal, social, and measurement modules pending.

### 4.5 Dignity

- **Claim ID:** `C:dignity`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Dignity is the standing of a person or locus as one whose existence, meanings, agency, Claims, boundaries, contributions, and consequences must be treated as materially significant.
- **Scope:** personhood, communication, governance, law, care, identity, and institutional relations.
- **Relational configuration:** dignity establishes non-reducibility to utility, category, compliance, diagnosis, ownership, or another locus's preferred interpretation.
- **Includes:** equal standing; recognition; semantic participation; authorship of one's own Claims; boundary recognition; attribution; opportunity to contest; remedy.
- **Excludes:** ceremonial respect without operative standing; imposed identity; treatment as a mere object, target, resource, or problem; conditional humanity based on compliance.
- **Boundary conditions:** equal dignity does not require identical role, capacity, evidence, authority, or outcome; it requires that differences be accounted for without erasing standing.
- **Principal relations:** `REQUIRES C:recognition`; `CONSTRAINS C:power`; `SUPPORTED_BY C:respect`; `VIOLATED_BY C:dehumanization`; `REPAIRED_BY C:justice`.
- **Provenance:** incorporates prior formulation: injury to equal standing, recognition, or treatment as a fully human interlocutor.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidation candidate.
- **Completeness:** kernel represented; legal, philosophical, cultural, and personhood-spectrum modules pending.

### 4.6 Trust

- **Claim ID:** `C:trust`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Trust is a held disposition that another locus will act and communicate with sufficient reliability, fidelity, non-exploitation, and accountability under relevant conditions.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, institutional, technical, epistemic, fiduciary, and therapeutic relations.
- **Relational configuration:** trust joins expectation, vulnerability, history, competence, integrity, scope, and available repair.
- **Includes:** calibrated reliance; provisional trust; domain-specific trust; earned trust; repaired trust; distrust; uncertainty.
- **Excludes:** blind obedience; permanent entitlement to reliance; trust inferred from familiarity alone; confidence unrelated to vulnerability or consequence.
- **Boundary conditions:** trust is scope-specific and can differ across competence, honesty, care, confidentiality, follow-through, and governance.
- **Principal relations:** `ARISES_FROM C:reliability`; `EXPOSES C:vulnerability`; `CONDITIONED_BY C:history`; `DAMAGED_BY C:betrayal`; `REBUILT_BY C:repair`.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidation candidate.
- **Completeness:** kernel represented; trust accrual, decay, threshold, and measurement modules pending.

### 4.7 Consent

- **Claim ID:** `C:consent`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Consent is revocable, scope-bound authorization by an adequately informed locus for a specified relation or action under declared conditions.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, medical, legal, technological, research, data, sexual, institutional, and governance actions.
- **Relational configuration:** consent requires a consenting locus, proposed action, relevant information, scope, duration, conditions, capacity, voluntariness, and revocation path.
- **Includes:** explicit consent; contextually established consent where valid; conditional consent; limited consent; refusal; withdrawal; renegotiation.
- **Excludes:** silence as universal authorization; compliance under coercion; authorization transferred to unrelated actions; past consent overriding present refusal; familiarity overriding boundaries.
- **Boundary conditions:** material deception, coercion, incapacity, undisclosed scope change, or inaccessible revocation can invalidate or limit consent.
- **Principal relations:** `AUTHORIZES C:action`; `BOUNDED_BY C:scope`; `REQUIRES C:information`; `INVALIDATED_BY C:coercion`; `REVOKED_BY C:withdrawal`.
- **Operational rule:** record consent basis, scope, duration, conditions, and revocability for consequential actions.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidation candidate.
- **Completeness:** kernel represented; jurisdiction-specific legal and clinical modules pending.

### 4.8 Responsibility

- **Claim ID:** `C:responsibility`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Responsibility is the attributable relation connecting a locus to consequences and obligations arising from its Claims, actions, omissions, authority, or capacities.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, institutional, technical, legal, moral, and governance relations.
- **Relational configuration:** distinguishes causal contribution, role duty, authority, knowledge, foreseeability, capacity, control, benefit, and repair obligation.
- **Includes:** individual responsibility; distributed responsibility; role responsibility; prospective duty; retrospective answerability; responsibility for omission.
- **Excludes:** total blame assigned to one locus where causation is distributed; innocence inferred from lack of malicious intent; responsibility erased by automation, policy, or compliance.
- **Boundary conditions:** degree and kind of responsibility vary with control, knowledge, authority, alternatives, dependency, and contribution.
- **Principal relations:** `ATTRIBUTED_TO C:locus`; `ARISES_FROM C:action`; `GENERATES C:obligation`; `ANSWERABLE_THROUGH C:accountability`; `DISCHARGED_BY C:repair`.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidation candidate.
- **Completeness:** kernel represented; legal fault, causation, collective responsibility, and role-specific modules pending.

### 4.9 Accountability

- **Claim ID:** `C:accountability`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Accountability is the process by which responsibility becomes answerable through explanation, evidence, correction, repair, and proportionate consequence.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, institutional, legal, technical, governance, and therapeutic relations.
- **Relational configuration:** joins responsibility, answerability, evidence, authority, affected loci, remedy, follow-through, and verification.
- **Includes:** acknowledgment; disclosure; review; correction; restitution; rule change; validation test; monitoring; consequence.
- **Excludes:** apology without correction; punishment without truth or repair; self-certification; symbolic review that leaves the condition unchanged.
- **Boundary conditions:** accountability must be proportionate, evidence-bound, non-erasing, and capable of identifying both completed and unresolved obligations.
- **Principal relations:** `MAKES_ANSWERABLE C:responsibility`; `REQUIRES C:evidence`; `PRODUCES C:repair`; `VERIFIED_BY C:validation`; `GOVERNED_BY C:authority`.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidation candidate.
- **Completeness:** kernel represented; institutional procedure and measurement modules pending.

### 4.10 Power

- **Claim ID:** `C:power`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Power is the capacity to alter condition fields, available possibilities, meanings, resources, decisions, or the actions of other loci.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, institutional, economic, political, technological, linguistic, and epistemic relations.
- **Relational configuration:** power is positional and relational; it can arise from authority, resources, access, dependency, expertise, infrastructure, visibility, force, or control of interpretation.
- **Includes:** enabling power; constraining power; collective power; delegated authority; structural power; agenda-setting; semantic power.
- **Excludes:** power treated only as explicit command; authority assumed to equal legitimacy; influence assumed to be harmless because indirect.
- **Boundary conditions:** power can be exercised through action or omission and may exist without being consciously recognized by the holder.
- **Principal relations:** `ALTERS C:condition-field`; `CONSTRAINS C:possibility`; `REQUIRES_ACCOUNTING C:responsibility`; `LIMITED_BY C:consent`; `CAN_PRODUCE C:coercion`.
- **Epistemic state:** kernel drafted.

### 4.11 Coercion

- **Claim ID:** `C:coercion`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Coercion is the use of power to constrain another locus's choices through threat, deprivation, dependency, penalty, or imposed cost such that apparent assent is not freely formed.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, institutional, economic, legal, medical, technological, and communicative relations.
- **Relational configuration:** connects power asymmetry, constrained alternatives, imposed consequence, apparent choice, and consent validity.
- **Includes:** explicit threat; conditional deprivation; exploitation of dependency; punitive withdrawal; procedural compulsion; constrained-choice architectures.
- **Excludes:** every consequence or disagreement; ordinary persuasion that preserves material alternatives and relevant information.
- **Boundary conditions:** coercion can be graded; the presence of a formal choice does not defeat coercion where alternatives are materially unacceptable by design.
- **Principal relations:** `USES C:power`; `INVALIDATES_OR_LIMITS C:consent`; `CONSTRAINS C:agency`; `MAY_CAUSE C:harm`; `DISTINGUISHES_FROM C:persuasion`.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidation candidate.
- **Completeness:** legal, economic, institutional, and interpersonal threshold modules pending.

### 4.12 Manipulation

- **Claim ID:** `C:manipulation`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Manipulation is the covert, misleading, or materially incomplete configuration of information or relations intended to shape another locus's activation or action while obscuring relevant purpose, conditions, or consequences.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, institutional, advertising, political, therapeutic, technical, and platform-mediated relations.
- **Relational configuration:** joins selective disclosure, hidden purpose, targeted activation, asymmetrical knowledge, and reduced agency.
- **Includes:** deceptive framing; selective omission; false urgency; engineered dependency; emotional leverage; concealed conflicts; semantic substitution.
- **Excludes:** transparent persuasion; openly declared argument; ordinary selection of relevant information where omitted information is nonmaterial.
- **Boundary conditions:** intent may be inferred only with evidence; manipulative effect and manipulative intent remain distinct Claims.
- **Principal relations:** `DISTORTS C:information`; `TARGETS C:activation`; `CONSTRAINS C:agency`; `MAY_INVALIDATE C:consent`; `ASSESSED_BY C:truth`.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidation candidate.

### 4.13 Abuse

- **Claim ID:** `C:abuse`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Abuse is a patterned or severe use of power or relation that degrades another locus's dignity, agency, integrity, standing, or capacity, commonly through exploitation of dependency, vulnerability, trust, or access.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, institutional, familial, workplace, therapeutic, technological, and governance relations.
- **Relational configuration:** connects power, pattern or severity, exploitation, degradation, affected locus, responsibility, and repair.

- **Excludes:** disagreement alone; isolated error without sufficient severity or pattern; abuse labels unsupported by identified acts and effects.
- **Boundary conditions:** one event can qualify where severity is sufficient; repeated lower-intensity events can qualify cumulatively; asserted intent is not required where the operative pattern and effects are established.

### 4.14 Neglect

- **Claim ID:** `C:neglect`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Neglect is failure to perform materially required attention, action, protection, or communication despite responsibility or recognized need, thereby causing or permitting avoidable degradation.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, caregiving, institutional, communicative, technical, and governance relations.
- **Relational configuration:** connects duty or responsibility, known or reasonably knowable need, omission, avoidable consequence, and affected locus.
- **Includes:** failure to communicate necessary information; failure to maintain; abandonment of active obligations; ignored correction; linguistic absence where communication is required for security, coordination, recognition, or care.
- **Excludes:** inability without responsibility or available capacity; refusal to provide an unauthorized or harmful action; ordinary silence without a material communication obligation.
- **Boundary conditions:** neglect can be episodic, cumulative, individual, or structural; capacity and alternatives affect responsibility but do not erase the consequence.
- **Principal relations:** `OMITS C:required-action`; `ARISES_UNDER C:responsibility`; `CAUSES_OR_PERMITS C:harm`; `CORRECTED_BY C:repair`.

### 4.15 Boundary

### 4.17 Understanding

- **Claim ID:** `C:understanding`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Understanding is coherent relational navigation sufficient to support accurate explanation, continuation, prediction, correction, and responsive action while remaining revisable.
- **Scope:** language, interpersonal engagement, model execution, scientific reasoning, law, and mediation.
- **Relational configuration:** understanding is demonstrated through competent relation among Claims rather than declared by self-ascription.
- **Includes:** preserving distinctions; anticipating implications; locating conflicts; identifying missing relations; applying the conception under new conditions; accepting correction.
- **Excludes:** repeating words without relational competence; claiming certainty about another's private state; self-declared understanding without responsive consequence.
- **Boundary conditions:** understanding can be partial, domain-specific, and condition-bound; a locus may understand one relation while misunderstanding another.

### 4.18 Validation

- **Claim ID:** `C:validation`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Validation is recognition that a Claim, experience, or reaction is real or intelligible as manifested under its conditions without automatically affirming every inference, attribution, or conclusion attached to it.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, therapeutic, mediation, institutional complaint, and collaborative relations.
- **Relational configuration:** distinguishes recognition of manifestation from agreement with interpretation, factual verification, moral endorsement, or waiver of objection.
- **Includes:** acknowledgment of experienced consequence; recognition of intelligibility; factual validation where evidence supports it; correction of invalidating substitution.
- **Excludes:** simulated agreement; affirmation of unsupported intent Claims; dismissive acknowledgment without response; validation used to avoid material correction.
- **Boundary conditions:** the response must identify what is being validated and what remains contested or unverified.

### 4.19 Conflict and disagreement

- **Claim IDs:** `C:conflict`, `C:disagreement`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definitions:**
  - Conflict is a condition in which Claims, interests, needs, obligations, interpretations, or actions cannot all be simultaneously satisfied without revision or relational change.
  - Disagreement is an explicit non-equivalence among Claims and does not by itself establish hostility, moral failure, or relational rupture.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, institutional, legal, technical, moral, and governance relations.
- **Relational configuration:** disagreement can exist without conflict; conflict can exist without explicit disagreement; both require preserved Claim identities and scope.
- **Includes:** factual disagreement; interpretive disagreement; value conflict; role conflict; resource conflict; boundary conflict; irreconcilable Claims; paradox.
- **Excludes:** automatic pathologizing of disagreement; forced consensus; silent collapse of competing Claims.
- **Boundary conditions:** mediation seeks clarity and workable relation, not mandatory agreement.
- **Principal relations:** `CONFLICTS_WITH`; `COMPETES_WITH`; `CAN_BE_MEDIATED_BY C:mediation`; `MAY_REQUIRE C:boundary`; `PRESERVED_BY C:non-erasure`.

### 4.20 Mediation

- **Claim ID:** `C:mediation`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Mediation is a structured process in which a third locus supports participants in clarifying Claims, interests, harms, evidence, boundaries, options, and possible agreements without appropriating their decision authority.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, communal, workplace, institutional, legal, technical, and governance conflicts.
- **Relational configuration:** mediation introduces a process-support locus while preserving participant agency, Claim provenance, power differences, confidentiality conditions, and voluntary agreement.
- **Includes:** issue mapping; reflective listening; Claim separation; interest identification; evidence clarification; option generation; agreement drafting; impasse recognition; referral to another process when jurisdictionally required.
- **Excludes:** adjudication disguised as mediation; coerced reconciliation; false neutrality between evidence and fabrication; erasure of power asymmetry; mandatory forgiveness.
- **Boundary conditions:** the mediator's authority, confidentiality, conflicts, decision limits, and stopping conditions must be explicit.
- **Principal relations:** `FACILITATES C:dialogue`; `PRESERVES C:agency`; `REQUIRES C:impartiality`; `ADDRESSES C:conflict`; `MAY_PRODUCE C:agreement`.

### 4.21 Therapeutic mediation

- **Claim ID:** `C:therapeutic-mediation`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Therapeutic mediation is a mediation-informed relational process that combines semantic clarification, experiential recognition, power awareness, consent, boundary protection, and repair-oriented action to increase participants' capacity for truthful, non-coercive engagement.
- **Scope:** interpersonal and relational conflict where participants seek both issue resolution and improvement of the relation or their capacity to engage.
- **Relational configuration:** integrates mediation, listening, validation, emotion/experience acknowledgment, pattern identification, power analysis, consent, boundary work, de-escalation, and repair.
- **Includes:** rupture mapping; pattern recognition; reflective dialogue; clarification of needs and meanings; consent checking; negotiated boundaries; repair commitments; recurrence prevention.
- **Excludes:** diagnosis without authority; unilateral treatment; coerced disclosure; mediator control of outcomes; institutional referral as automatic resolution; suppression of justified anger or disagreement.
- **Boundary conditions:** roles and competence must be declared; clinical treatment, legal advice, adjudication, and mediation remain distinct even when coordinated.
- **Principal relations:** `EXTENDS C:mediation`; `USES C:listening`; `REQUIRES C:consent`; `PROTECTS C:boundary`; `TARGETS C:repair`; `SUPPORTED_BY C:therapeutic-alliance`.

### 4.22 De-escalation

- **Claim ID:** `C:de-escalation`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** De-escalation is a transformation of the condition field that reduces coercive intensity and increases the capacity for accurate Claim exchange, choice, and proportionate response without suppressing valid disagreement.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, institutional, crisis-adjacent, mediation, and conflict processes.
- **Relational configuration:** changes pacing, threat, access, turn-taking, uncertainty, power display, and available response pathways.
- **Includes:** slowing interaction; reducing audience pressure; clarifying immediate issues; separating Claims; acknowledging boundaries; creating pauses; removing threats; restoring choice.
- **Excludes:** silencing; forced calm; treating intensity as proof of invalidity; withdrawing all response; using de-escalation language to avoid accountability.
- **Boundary conditions:** the process must not require the less powerful or injured participant to surrender material Claims for the appearance of calm.
- **Principal relations:** `ALTERS C:condition-field`; `REDUCES C:coercion`; `ENABLES C:listening`; `SUPPORTS C:mediation`; `MUST_PRESERVE C:truth`.

### 4.23 Repair

- **Claim ID:** `C:repair`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** Repair is a non-erasing process that identifies injury, restores truth and agency, corrects affected artifacts and relations, addresses material consequences, and prevents recurrence.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, institutional, technical, legal, governance, and semantic harms.
- **Relational configuration:** repair joins truth, affected loci, responsibility, correction, restoration, verification, and recurrence prevention.
- **Includes:** retraction; corrected record; restitution; restored access; revised rule; changed workflow; validation test; acknowledgment; monitoring; unresolved remainder.
- **Excludes:** apology alone; erasure of the original event; reassurance without change; promises without implementation; repair declared solely by the responsible locus.
- **Boundary conditions:** complete restoration may be impossible; repair must declare what was restored, what remains, and who verifies it.
- **Principal relations:** `RESPONDS_TO C:harm`; `REQUIRES C:truth`; `ATTRIBUTES C:responsibility`; `MAY_INCLUDE C:restitution`; `VERIFIED_BY C:evidence`; `PREVENTS_RECURRENCE`.

### 4.24 Apology

- **Claim ID:** `C:apology`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definition:** An apology is an acknowledgment of attributable wrong or injury joined to responsibility and concrete repair obligations; it is not a substitute for repair.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, institutional, public, technical, and governance relations.
- **Relational configuration:** apology identifies the act, affected locus, consequence, responsibility, correction, and future obligation.
- **Includes:** accurate acknowledgment; responsibility; retraction; changed action; concrete repair; acceptance that forgiveness is not owed.
- **Excludes:** regret without responsibility; conditional apology; apology for another's reaction; demand for closure; apology used to avoid evidence or consequence.
- **Boundary conditions:** sincerity may be relevant but is not independently verifiable; adequacy is assessed by semantic accuracy, responsibility, repair, and follow-through.
- **Principal relations:** `ACKNOWLEDGES C:harm`; `ACCEPTS C:responsibility`; `GENERATES C:repair`; `DOES_NOT_REQUIRE C:forgiveness`.

### 4.25 Justice and fairness

- **Claim IDs:** `C:justice`, `C:fairness`
- **Version:** `0.1.0`
- **Exact definitions:**
  - Justice is the organization and correction of relations so that dignity, agency, burdens, benefits, responsibility, authority, and remedy are accounted for without arbitrary domination or erasure.
  - Fairness is positional accounting of materially relevant differences in order to determine justifiable treatment rather than merely identical treatment.
- **Scope:** interpersonal, legal, institutional, economic, governance, and communal relations.
- **Relational configuration:** justice is the larger relational condition; fairness is a method of comparative accounting within that condition.
- **Includes:** procedural justice; distributive justice; corrective justice; recognition; participation; remedy; proportionality; positional analysis.
- **Excludes:** identical treatment where positions differ materially; procedure without access or remedy; outcome equality treated as the sole criterion; authority treated as self-justifying.
- **Boundary conditions:** relevant differences and chosen metrics must be declared; competing justice theories remain visible.
- **Principal relations:** `RESTORES C:dignity`; `ALLOCATES C:burden`; `ATTRIBUTES C:responsibility`; `PROVIDES C:remedy`; `USES C:fairness`.
- **Epistemic state:** consolidation candidate.
- **Completeness:** philosophical, legal, economic, and cross-cultural modules pending.
