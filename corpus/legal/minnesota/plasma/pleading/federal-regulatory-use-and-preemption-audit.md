# Minnesota Plasma Matter — Federal Regulatory Use, Jurisdiction, and Preemption Audit

Matter: `MAT-PLASMA-2026-08-04-001`
Status: filing-control document; not evidence and not a legal opinion.
Verification date: 2026-08-05.

## Purpose

This audit controls how federal blood and Source Plasma regulations may be used in a Minnesota state-law complaint. It prevents the draft from converting federal regulations into an unsupported private federal cause of action, overstating medical-supervision requirements, or creating avoidable federal-question and preemption disputes.

## Governing controls

1. Plead Minnesota causes of action only unless a separately verified federal cause of action is identified.
2. Use federal regulations as potentially relevant evidence of regulatory context, materiality, record existence, notice, or standard-of-care issues only after the precise subsection, event-date text, applicability, and evidentiary use are verified.
3. Do not allege that a regulatory violation automatically establishes civil liability.
4. Do not characterize the term `donor` as proof that the transaction is uncompensated. Federal regulations expressly regulate Source Plasma from paid donors.
5. Separate requirements imposed on an establishment, responsible physician, physician substitute, and other trained personnel.
6. Quote or paraphrase only the exact provision applicable to the collection type and event date.
7. Preserve preemption, primary-jurisdiction, and federal-question defenses in the defense matrix rather than assuming they are unavailable.

## Medical-supervision pleading control

The complaint must not plead a categorical `no physician physically present` theory as a regulatory violation unless a provision applicable to the exact event required physical presence. The legally safer factual sequence is:

1. identify the activity performed;
2. identify who performed it and that person’s documented role;
3. identify the applicable regulatory provision;
4. determine whether the activity was delegable;
5. obtain the delegation, qualification, SOP, and responsible-physician records;
6. allege only the supported defect, such as absent determination, impermissible delegation, deficient documentation, or failure to follow the applicable procedure.

Any broader `signature-only oversight is categorically insufficient` proposition remains `HOLD` pending authority and event-specific proof.

## Federal-jurisdiction control

| Risk | Trigger in draft | Required treatment | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Federal-question characterization | A count is framed as arising directly under the FDCA or federal blood regulations | Remove unless an express or otherwise cognizable federal cause of action is separately verified | CONTROLLED |
| Embedded federal issue | State claim necessarily turns on a disputed, substantial federal issue | Draft the state-law duty and deception theory independently where legally supportable; record removal analysis before service | OPEN |
| Complete-preemption assertion | Defendant argues federal law displaces the state claim | Research the specific statute, claim, and remedy; do not concede complete preemption by implication | OPEN |
| Ordinary conflict or obstacle preemption | Requested state remedy allegedly conflicts with FDA requirements or regulatory objectives | Narrow relief to supported disclosures, records, and state-law remedies that do not command federally prohibited conduct | OPEN |
| Primary jurisdiction | Defendant requests agency referral or stay | Distinguish issues requiring agency expertise from conventional misrepresentation, transaction, causation, and damages issues | OPEN |

## Complaint edits required before filing

- Every reference to 21 C.F.R. parts 606, 630, or 640 must identify a precise section and its proposition.
- Replace categorical assertions that physician absence alone violates federal law with activity-specific delegation and documentation allegations.
- Do not allege that use of the word `donation` is false solely because compensation was paid; tie any deception theory to the complete representation, omitted commercial facts, medical-risk information, and evidence of materiality.
- Do not request an injunction that requires conduct inconsistent with an FDA license, approved program, or federal regulation without a developed conflict analysis.
- Add an express pleading control that federal regulations are not asserted as an independent private cause of action.
- Link every regulatory allegation to an event, evidence ID, event-date authority record, and Rule 11 map row.

## Filing gate

No federal regulatory proposition may enter the filing copy unless the exact current and event-date provision is verified, applicability is mapped to the collection and participant category, the relevant actor and duty are identified, supporting evidence is linked, contrary exceptions are addressed, and the complaint makes clear that the provision is not being pleaded as an unsupported independent private cause of action.
