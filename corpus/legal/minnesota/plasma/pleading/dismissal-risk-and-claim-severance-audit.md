# Dismissal-Risk and Claim-Severance Audit

Status: filing-control document; not evidence and not a legal opinion.

## Purpose

This audit identifies defects that could cause dismissal, sanctions, claim loss, or avoidable procedural entanglement. It separates claims that may proceed on documentary and participant evidence from claims that likely require expert review, additional factual specificity, or separate remedial treatment.

## 1. Immediate pleading risks

| Risk | Current condition | Required control | Filing gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unidentified defendants | Complaint uses bracketed entity fields | Confirm exact legal entity, trade name, facility operator, registered agent, and facility address for each defendant | BLOCKED |
| Unverified event dates | Participation allegations are not tied to dated records | Build event chronology from receipts, payment history, donor portal records, laboratory records, messages, and declarations | BLOCKED |
| Fraud particularity | Representations and omissions are described categorically | State speaker, defendant, medium, date or bounded period, exact or faithful language, materiality, exposure, reliance or causal connection, and injury | BLOCKED |
| Public-benefit showing | Complaint alleges standardized conduct but lacks supporting exhibits | Attach or identify standardized advertisements, consent forms, policies, compensation schedules, and multi-participant reach evidence | PARTIAL |
| Causation | Physiological and laboratory consequences are not record-linked | Separate documented injury from risk exposure; obtain records and expert analysis before asserting medical causation | BLOCKED |
| Damages methodology | Structural-relief amount is expressly conditional | Do not plead a fixed pool as established damages; retain only as a proposal contingent on admissible statewide proof and legal authority | HOLD |
| Venue | Hennepin County basis remains conditional | Tie at least one transaction, defendant business location, or controlling venue statute to Hennepin County | BLOCKED |
| Service | Defendants and registered agents are unknown | Complete summons, service address, authorized recipient, and proof-of-service plan per defendant | BLOCKED |

## 2. Claim-severance architecture

### Track A — Consumer-protection claims

Counts I and II may be evaluated separately from medical-negligence theories when supported by participant-facing advertisements, compensation representations, consent materials, testing statements, frequency statements, and omissions.

Required minimum proof package:

1. Defendant-specific representation or omission.
2. Date or bounded period.
3. Source copy, screenshot, transcription, receipt, portal record, or declaration.
4. Materiality explanation.
5. Plaintiff exposure and transaction connection.
6. Concrete injury or loss.
7. Evidence that the challenged practice was directed beyond a private one-off dispute when relying on Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a public-benefit doctrine.

Control: Do not use “statutory enforcement relator” as though it confers Attorney General status. Plead Plaintiff as a private person invoking the private remedy in Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a.

### Track B — Medical-negligence and informed-consent claims

Counts III and IV must remain on hold unless the filing package resolves whether expert testimony is necessary and, if so, complies with Minn. Stat. § 145.682.

Current statutory control:

- A pro se plaintiff signs the expert-review affidavit and is bound as an attorney would be.
- When expert testimony is necessary, the initial affidavit generally must be served with the summons and complaint.
- A limitations-based exception permits later service only under the statute’s stated conditions.
- Noncompliance can produce mandatory dismissal with prejudice of claims requiring expert testimony.

Required gate:

1. Identify each alleged standard-of-care deviation.
2. Identify the health-care-provider defendant to whom the deviation is attributed.
3. Determine whether expert testimony is necessary for breach and causation.
4. Obtain a qualified expert review.
5. Prepare the initial affidavit before service unless the statutory exception is properly invoked.
6. Calendar the later expert-identification affidavit deadline from commencement of discovery.

Control: If expert review is unavailable, remove or expressly withhold claims that require it rather than filing them unsupported.

### Track C — Unjust enrichment and equitable relief

Count V is pleaded in the alternative. It should not duplicate an adequate legal remedy without explaining the distinct benefit and equitable basis.

Count VI must tie each requested injunction to:

- an identified continuing practice;
- Plaintiff standing to seek prospective relief;
- evidence of likely recurrence or ongoing exposure;
- administrable terms;
- legal authority for the court to order the specific mechanism.

Control: Administrator appointment under Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3c is textually linked to Attorney General actions. Do not assume a private plaintiff can demand the same statutory administrator mechanism. Any private remedial administrator or monitoring proposal must be supported by independent equitable authority.

## 3. Commencement and filing sequence

Minnesota civil actions are generally commenced by service of the summons and complaint, not merely by filing. After service, the initiating papers, proof or waiver of service, filing fee or fee-waiver materials, and civil cover sheet must be filed. Rule 5.04 timing must be separately calendared.

Operational sequence:

1. Lock final defendant identities and service recipients.
2. Complete limitations and accrual audit for every event and claim.
3. Complete expert-review gate before serving any complaint containing expert-dependent claims.
4. Finalize summons and complaint.
5. Complete privacy review and redact protected identifiers.
6. Serve each defendant using an authorized method.
7. Preserve proof of service.
8. File required initiating papers, proof or waiver, cover sheet, and fee or waiver request.
9. Calendar response deadlines, Rule 5.04, expert-affidavit deadlines, and all court notices.

## 4. Sentence-level correction directives

Before filing, revise the working complaint as follows:

- Replace all bracketed party and contact fields.
- Replace “approximately” and categorical assertions with record-supported dates, ranges, and sources.
- Attribute every defendant-specific act.
- Distinguish personal observation, staff statement, document content, inference, and information-and-belief.
- Remove any allegation that cannot be supported after reasonable inquiry.
- Separate risk exposure from diagnosed injury.
- Do not characterize federal regulation as creating a private federal cause of action.
- Do not equate plasma downstream value with compensatory damages.
- Do not state a statewide participant count, market value, or relief amount without methodology and admissible sources.
