# Damages, Restitution, Fees, and Structural-Relief Methodology

## Purpose

This file controls the monetary and equitable relief theories in the Minnesota compensated-plasma working complaint. It is a filing gate, not evidence and not a damages opinion. No number may enter the complaint, demand, affidavit, settlement communication, or public statement unless its source, legal basis, calculation method, attribution, and uncertainty are recorded here or in a linked schedule.

## Governing control principles

1. Plaintiff-specific compensatory damages must be separated from statewide transaction value, defendant revenue, downstream plasma value, disgorgement, restitution, civil penalties, punitive damages, attorney fees, costs, medical-monitoring expense, and structural-remediation cost.
2. Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3a authorizes an injured person to seek damages, costs and disbursements including investigation costs and reasonable attorney fees, and other equitable relief as determined by the court. It does not make every requested monetary measure recoverable and does not eliminate causation, proof, standing, public-benefit, proportionality, or anti-duplication requirements.
3. The administrator mechanism in Minn. Stat. § 8.31, subd. 3c is not assumed available in a private-plaintiff action.
4. Defendant gross revenue, industry market size, downstream product value, or statewide plasma volume cannot be pleaded as Plaintiff's compensatory loss without a legally valid conversion theory and admissible evidence.
5. No punitive-damages claim may be inserted into the initial pleading unless current Minnesota procedure authorizes it. Any later punitive-damages motion must satisfy the applicable prima-facie and substantive standards, including Minn. Stat. §§ 549.191 and 549.20.
6. Equitable monetary relief must be classified by legal theory. Restitution, unjust enrichment, disgorgement, rescission, surcharge, constructive trust, and refund are not interchangeable labels.
7. Medical monitoring, independent assessment, notice, audit, reporting, and record-preservation relief require separate standing, causation, authority, scope, cost, administrability, and non-duplication findings.

## Compensation and offset control

Payments received for plasma participation must be preserved and analyzed; they cannot be silently omitted. The damages model must state whether each payment is:

- transaction consideration relevant to reliance and loss;
- an offset against a claimed economic measure;
- evidence of inducement or standardized practice;
- irrelevant to a distinct physical-injury measure; or
- disputed in treatment under applicable law.

No single treatment applies automatically across all claims.

## Structural-relief pool control

The proposed figure of not less than $3.6 billion remains a research hypothesis only. It must not be presented as an adjudicated liability, verified damages amount, settlement value, or filing-ready demand.

A supportable structural-relief calculation requires:

1. **Authorized remedy components.** Identify each component separately: notice, assessment, testing, monitoring, treatment, restitution, administration, audit, reporting, record preservation, or other relief.
2. **Eligible population.** Define who qualifies, by defendant, facility, date range, participation frequency, exposure, injury, and jurisdiction.
3. **Participation denominator.** Distinguish visits, procedures, unique persons, liters collected, centers, and years.
4. **Unit cost.** Obtain documented market or contractual cost per assessment, test, notice, audit, administrator hour, or other component.
5. **Utilization assumption.** State expected uptake and justify it with evidence; show low, central, and high scenarios.
6. **Duration.** Identify treatment, monitoring, audit, and claims-administration periods.
7. **Causation allocation.** Exclude unrelated conditions, facilities, periods, and defendants.
8. **Offsets and overlap.** Prevent duplicate recovery across compensation, insurance, restitution, medical expense, settlements, and other programs.
9. **Present value and inflation.** State date, discount rate, inflation basis, and sensitivity where future costs are included.
10. **Administration.** Specify governance, eligibility review, appeals, privacy, audit, unclaimed funds, termination, and court supervision.
11. **Authority.** Identify the source of judicial power for each component; do not rely on remedial desirability alone.
12. **Proportionality and redressability.** Link the remedy to proven conduct and Plaintiff's cognizable injury.

### Structural model template

`Total authorized structural cost = Σ (eligible population × utilization rate × unit cost × frequency × duration) + documented administration + documented audit/reporting - offsets - duplication`

The model must publish all assumptions and provide low, central, and high scenarios. Industry revenue or downstream plasma value may be contextual evidence but cannot replace this costed remedial model.

## Punitive-damages control

Punitive damages are excluded from the current complaint. Any later request requires:

- a motion and evidentiary record satisfying the procedure then in force;
- clear defendant-specific evidence of deliberate disregard;
- principal or managerial-agent attribution where organizational liability is sought;
- separation from compensatory proof and financial-condition material;
- analysis of other punishment and duplicate punitive exposure;
- a proposed amendment that does not contaminate the initial pleading with prohibited allegations.

## Fees, costs, and investigation ledger

Create `FEE-###` entries containing date, actor, task, time, rate, amount, invoice/receipt, claim relation, necessity, and recoverability classification. Self-represented time must not be assigned an attorney-fee value without authority. Fee-shifting eligibility does not establish the amount or reasonableness of an award.

## Anti-duplication matrix

Before finalizing the prayer for relief, test every pair of requested remedies for overlapping measures. At minimum compare:

- compensatory damages versus restitution;
- restitution versus unjust enrichment/disgorgement;
- past medical expense versus monitoring fund;
- future medical expense versus structural assessment and treatment;
- statutory damages versus common-law damages;
- defendant gain versus statewide market value;
- attorney fees versus investigation costs and taxable costs;
- individual recovery versus group-directed administration;
- punitive damages versus civil penalties or other punishment.

Any overlap must be eliminated, pleaded expressly in the alternative, or accompanied by a non-duplication instruction.

## Required evidence and discovery targets

- participant payment histories and compensation schedules;
- facility-level transaction and unique-participant counts;
- collection-volume and procedure-frequency records;
- defendant revenue, cost, transfer-pricing, and affiliate-payment records limited to a legally relevant measure;
- plaintiff receipts, banking records, transit records, medical bills, laboratory records, and work-loss records;
- costs of testing, assessment, monitoring, notice, audit, reporting, and administration;
- insurance, reimbursement, refund, settlement, and other offset records;
- evidence supporting defendant knowledge, duration, profitability, concealment, managerial involvement, and response to identified risk, if punitive damages are later investigated.

## Complaint revisions required before filing

1. Replace the phrase `restitution, disgorgement, or other equitable monetary relief where authorized` with claim-specific alternatives tied to identified authority and proof, or retain it only as expressly alternative relief.
2. Remove or withhold the $3.6 billion structural-relief figure unless the full methodology, authority, and evidence gates above are satisfied.
3. State only plaintiff-specific damages presently supported by records.
4. Exclude punitive damages from the initial complaint unless current Minnesota procedure and evidence permit inclusion.
5. Define each requested injunction with actor, prohibited or required conduct, affected population, duration, reporting, enforcement, and termination terms.
6. Reconcile all monetary relief with compensation received and other offsets without conceding an offset where law does not require one.
